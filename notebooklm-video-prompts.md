## FP512 — Risk, Insurance & Benefits

### M5 — Health, Medicare & Medicaid

```
Make a ~10-minute high-yield exam-review video for a CFP® candidate doing final review of Health Insurance & Government Programs. Assume they know the basics — focus on the most heavily tested points and the traps that cost marks. Prioritize: HMO vs PPO vs POS vs HDHP designs and cost-sharing; the HSA (requires an HDHP, triple-tax-advantaged, contribution limits); COBRA (~18 months, employers with 20+ employees, retiree pays full premium + 2%); and Medicare Parts A (hospital), B (outpatient), C (Advantage), D (drugs). Draw two distinctions clearly: Medicare Part C vs Part D, and Medicare vs Medicaid for long-term custodial care. Hammer the #1 trap: Medicare does NOT cover long-term custodial care — Medicaid does, after spend-down. Define each term the first time, use concrete numbers, and end with the 4–5 things most likely to appear on test day. Skip history and filler; keep it fast and visual.
```

### M8 — Business Risk Solutions

```
Make a ~10-minute high-yield exam-review video for a CFP® candidate doing final review of Business Uses of Insurance. Assume they know the basics — focus on the tested distinctions and traps. Prioritize: cross-purchase vs entity (stock-redemption) buy-sell agreements — cross-purchase gives surviving owners a BASIS STEP-UP but needs many policies [n(n−1)], while entity-purchase needs fewer policies (n) but gives NO basis step-up; key-person insurance (the business is owner, payer, and beneficiary — premiums are NOT deductible, proceeds generally income-tax-free); and split-dollar / business-continuation arrangements. Hammer the two traps: claiming entity-purchase gives a basis step-up (only cross-purchase does), and deducting key-person or buy-sell premiums (they're not deductible). Give a rule of thumb: few owners + want basis step-up → cross-purchase; many owners → entity. End with the 4–5 highest-yield points. Keep it fast and visual.
```

## FP511 — General Planning, Conduct & Psychology

### M1 — Personal Financial Planning

```
Make a ~10-minute high-yield exam-review video for a CFP® candidate doing final review of the Personal Financial Planning process. Focus on the most tested points and traps. Prioritize: the 7-step financial planning process IN ORDER (1 Understand the client, 2 Identify & select goals, 3 Analyze the current course, 4 Develop recommendations, 5 Present recommendations, 6 Implement, 7 Monitor) — this frames nearly every scenario question; the fact that a CFP® professional is a fiduciary AT ALL TIMES when giving financial advice (not just for investments); the disclosures that must be in writing (material conflicts, compensation, scope of engagement); and the financial life-cycle phases (Accumulation → Conservation → Distribution). Call out the traps: reordering the steps (Analyze comes before developing recommendations), thinking fiduciary duty applies only to investments, and skipping ongoing monitoring. Teach the "what is the planner's NEXT step?" approach. End with the 4–5 highest-yield points. Keep it fast and visual.
```

### M2 — Psychology of Financial Planning

```
Make a ~10-minute high-yield exam-review video for a CFP® candidate doing final review of the Psychology of Financial Planning. Focus on what's tested and the traps. Prioritize: the common behavioral biases (anchoring, overconfidence, recency, loss aversion, framing, herding) with a one-line tell for each; the Klontz money scripts (money avoidance, money worship, money status, money vigilance) as beliefs formed early in life; the difference between risk TOLERANCE (willingness) and risk CAPACITY (ability to absorb loss); and counseling techniques (active listening, open-ended questions, empathy). Note that loss aversion makes losses feel about twice as painful as equal gains, so clients hold losers and sell winners. Hammer the two traps: confusing tolerance with capacity, and mislabeling anchoring vs recency. Teach the "emotional/irrational decision in the stem → name the bias" pattern. End with the 4–5 highest-yield points. Keep it fast and visual.
```

### M3 — Financial Statements & Cash Flow

```
Make a ~10-minute high-yield exam-review video for a CFP® candidate doing final review of Financial Statements and Cash Flow. Focus on tested points and traps. Prioritize: the statement of financial position (assets at fair market value vs liabilities at outstanding balance; net worth = assets − liabilities); the cash-flow statement (inflows vs outflows, split fixed vs discretionary); and the core ratios — emergency fund = 3–6 months of FIXED (nondiscretionary) expenses, housing ratio ≤ 28% of GROSS income (PITI), total debt ratio ≤ 36% of GROSS income, target savings rate ~10–20%. Hammer the traps: computing the emergency fund off total expenses instead of fixed expenses, using NET income for the 28/36 ratios instead of gross, and listing an asset at original cost instead of current FMV. Teach "on a ratio question, check the denominator." Work one quick ratio example. End with the 4–5 highest-yield points. Keep it fast and visual.
```

### M4 — Time Value of Money

```
Make a ~10-minute high-yield exam-review video for a CFP® candidate doing final review of Time Value of Money. Focus on tested mechanics and traps, and SHOW the calculator logic. Prioritize: FV/PV of single sums and ordinary annuity vs annuity due; the real (inflation-adjusted) rate using the formula [(1+nominal)/(1+inflation) − 1] — not simple subtraction; setting BEG vs END mode (BEG when the first cash flow is today); NPV and IRR with the decision rule (accept if NPV ≥ 0 / IRR ≥ required return); the Rule of 72; the serial payment (grows with inflation); and how more frequent compounding raises the effective rate. Hammer the traps: subtracting inflation instead of using the real-rate formula, leaving the calculator in END mode for an annuity-due problem, and confusing nominal vs effective rates. Teach: "in today's dollars / inflation-adjusted → use the real rate." Walk one worked keystroke sequence. End with the 4–5 highest-yield points. Keep it fast and visual.
```

### M5 — Professional Conduct & Fiduciary

```
Make a ~10-minute high-yield exam-review video for a CFP® candidate doing final review of Professional Conduct and Fiduciary Responsibility (the CFP Board Code and Standards). Focus on tested points and traps. Prioritize: the duties owed to each party (Client, Firm, Public, and CFP Board); the fiduciary duty and its three parts (Duty of Loyalty, Duty of Care, and Duty to Follow Client Instructions) — owed AT ALL TIMES when giving financial advice; matching a fact pattern to the Standard of Conduct it violates; and the discipline/sanction ladder (private censure → public letter of admonition → suspension → revocation of the marks). ### M6 — Economic Environment & Consumer Protection

```
Make a ~10-minute high-yield exam-review video for a CFP® candidate doing final review of the Economic Environment and Consumer Protection. Focus on tested points and traps. Prioritize: the business cycle phases (Expansion → Peak → Contraction → Trough); monetary policy (the Federal Reserve — open-market operations, the discount rate, reserve requirements) vs fiscal policy (Congress — taxing and spending); how the Fed RAISES rates to fight inflation and LOWERS them to stimulate; the yield curve (normal/upward vs inverted, which often precedes a recession); and the major consumer-protection laws — TILA (disclosure), FCRA (credit reports), ECOA (no discrimination in lending), and Fair Debt Collection. Hammer the traps: attributing open-market operations to Congress (it's the Fed) and mixing up which consumer law covers what. Teach the tell: "Fed in the stem → monetary; Congress/tax → fiscal." End with the 4–5 highest-yield points. Keep it fast and visual.
```

### M8 — Case Study

```
Make a ~10-minute exam-strategy video for a CFP® candidate on how to attack integrated CASE-STUDY questions (this module pulls insurance, tax, investment, retirement, and estate together). Instead of teaching one topic, teach the method: read the full fact pattern first and note which planning domains it touches; spot cross-domain overlaps (estate + tax, insurance + retirement, investment + tax); prioritize recommendations by the client's stated goals and biggest risk exposures; and remember the exam asks "what should the planner DO?" — translate concepts into actions.
