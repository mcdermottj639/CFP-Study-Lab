Learning Objective 7.1.1

Group life insurance & its taxation

🛡️Section 79 — what makes a group life plan qualify

A Section 79 group term life (GTL) plan has three basic policy requirements:

  • Must provide a general death benefit
  • Must be provided to a group of employees (all employees, or a class based on age, marital status, or work-related factors — duties, compensation, length of service)
  • Coverage amount must be based on a uniform formula for all employees

Typical GTL features: annual renewable term, face amount paid to beneficiary, coverage based on compensation/position/service or a flat amount — low cost, tax-advantaged, simple administration, no medical exam, premium waiver.

✅The four nondiscrimination "qualify" tests

A plan must meet one of the following four requirements to qualify:

  1. At least 70% of employees must benefit
  2. At least 85% of plan participants are not key employees
  3. Plan benefits a nondiscriminatory classification of employees
  4. Complies with IRC §125 requirements if part of a cafeteria plan

Memory hookOnly one of the four tests must be satisfied — not all four. The 70%/85% figures are the most-tested numbers.

💵The $50,000 line — GTL taxation

Employer-provided GTL is tax-free to the employee up to $50,000 of coverage. The cost of coverage over $50,000 becomes taxable imputed income to the employee, valued using the IRS Table I uniform-premium rates (not the employer's actual cost).

Income-tax treatment of GTL by coverage layer
Coverage layerEmployer deductionEmployee income
Up to $50,000Deductible (nonbeneficiary)Nontaxable to employee
Over $50,000Deductible (nonbeneficiary)Taxable imputed income*

*Employee contributions toward coverage are deductible from the cost of employer-paid coverage over $50,000. Exceptions: the excess cost is not taxable if the beneficiary is a charity or the employer, or if the covered employee is disabled or a certain retired person.

🧮Worked example — Table I imputed income

John is 52 and his company provides $250,000 of group term life as part of his benefits. What is the annual tax impact of this benefit on John?

Imputed-income calculation

1
Subtract the $50,000 tax-free layer.
$250,000 − $50,000 = $200,000 excess coverage
2
Convert the excess to units of $1,000.
$200,000 ÷ $1,000 = 200 units
3
Look up John's Table I rate (age 52 → 50–54 bracket).
Rate = $0.23 per $1,000 per month
4
Multiply, then annualize.
200 × $0.23 = $46.00 / month · × 12 = $552.00 / year
Imputed income added to John's W-2 ≈ $46.00/month → $552.00/year.
MethodAlways subtract $50,000 first, divide by 1,000, multiply by the Table I monthly rate — and remember to annualize (× 12) if the question asks for the yearly amount.

👥Types of group life coverage

Group life insurance coverage types
TypeKey features
Group carve-outIndividual, discriminatory benefits for selected (older, higher-paid) executives removed from GTL coverage; can help the GTL plan come into compliance. Nondiscrimination does not apply; tax consequences to the employee.
Dependent group lifeCovers spouse & unmarried children; generally only with employee coverage. Coverage limited to $2,000; spouse coverage convertible.
Supplemental group lifeContributory group term for a class of (or all) employees, not selected individuals. Generally requires evidence of insurability.

Exam trapsDependent group life is capped at $2,000. The group carve-out pulls older/higher-paid execs OUT of GTL, and nondiscrimination rules don't apply to them.

🔑Key employee life insurance

Insurance on executives / highly valued employees; the policy is owned by, applied for by, and payable to the company.

Premiums NOT deductible
The company cannot deduct premiums on key-employee coverage.
Death benefit tax-free
Proceeds received by the company are generally income-tax-free.
Company-owned
Company owns, applies for, and is beneficiary of the policy.
Most-tested factKey-employee life premiums are NOT tax-deductible — this is the classic "which is NOT correct" answer.

🔁Conversion of GTL coverage

GTL typically carries a conversion provision to a permanent cash-value policy on leaving the group:

  • Conversion is guaranteed (no new evidence of insurability)
  • The permanent-policy premium is higher than the group term rate

Types of group health coverage

🏥Managed-care & supplemental coverages

PPO
Preferred Provider Plan — network of providers; out-of-network allowed at higher cost.
POS
Point of Service Plan — hybrid of HMO gatekeeping and PPO flexibility.
HMO
Health Maintenance Organization — in-network care through a primary-care gatekeeper.

Group vision: covers eye exams, eyeglasses, contact lenses and other corrective items; network of providers; usually limited benefits.

🦷Group dental & group LTC

Group dental typically covers routine care — cleanings, fillings, crowns, root canals, orthodontia. Many plans are PPOs today (not indemnity). Oral surgery is generally covered under health insurance, not dental.

Group LTC vs. individual LTC insurance
Group LTCIndividual LTC
Less expensive; group rates; lower ratesCan be customized to individual needs
No evidence of insurability; similar coverage & benefitsFully tailored per person
May extend to other family members; continued at termination/retirement via direct pay (no employer premium contribution)Portable by nature
DistinguishGroup LTC's edge = no evidence of insurability and lower cost. Individual LTC's edge = it can be customized. Employers cannot tailor group LTC per employee.

Learning Objective 7.2.2

Funding methods & tax-advantaged accounts

💼Employer funding & Section 125 cafeteria plans

Funding methods: fully insured (traditional) · self-insured · combination.

A Section 125 cafeteria plan lets employees choose among qualified benefits, including a cash option; elected qualified benefits are taken pretax.

NOT cafeteria-eligibleScholarships & fellowships, educational assistance, employee discounts, and retirement / nonqualified plan benefits are not qualified cafeteria-plan benefits.

🧾Flexible Spending Accounts (FSA) & DCAP

  • Pretax contributions (federal, state, FICA) through the employer
  • 2025 limit: $3,300
  • Use-it-or-lose-it — employer may allow either a $660 rollover into next year, or a grace period paying prior-year balance in the first 2½ months
  • Covers eligible medical expenses, but not OTC
  • DCAP — dependent-care expenses are FSA-eligible; a self-employed person is not eligible for an FSA

💰Health Savings Accounts (HSA) — 2025 limits

Funded by the participant, employer, or both; unused balances accrue and are not tied to the employer (unlike FSAs). Contributions can be invested (stocks, bonds, index funds, ETFs, mutual funds).

HSA / HDHP 2025 figures (single / family)
ItemSingleFamily
Maximum annual contribution$4,300$8,550
HDHP minimum deductible$1,650$3,300
HDHP maximum out-of-pocket$8,300$16,600

Tax character: qualified medical expenses are always tax- and penalty-free. Nonmedical withdrawals = ordinary income tax + 20% penalty; at age 65+ nonmedical use is ordinary income tax but no penalty. Triple tax advantage makes the HSA a strong supplemental retirement vehicle.

FSA vs. HSAFSA = use-it-or-lose-it and tied to the employer. HSA = portable, rolls over, invests, and outlives the job.

VehicleKey rules (2025)

🤝VEBAs

A Voluntary Employees' Beneficiary Association is a tax-exempt trust established by the employer to fund employee benefits. Some benefits are not eligible to be provided through a VEBA.

Regulation & taxation of group health

⚖️ERISA

The Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA) protects benefit-plan participants, establishes fiduciary standards, and imposes reporting & disclosure requirements.

ERISA reporting & disclosure responsibilities
Employee communicationsGovernment reporting
Summary Annual Report (SAR)Form 5500
Summary Plan Description (SPD)Form 5330
Summary of Material Modifications (SMM)Form 1099-R
Personal benefits statements · periodic pension benefit statements
Scope trapMost employer-sponsored group life plans must meet ERISA's reporting, disclosure, and fiduciary provisions — but they are NOT subject to participation/vesting or plan-termination-insurance rules (those apply to pensions).

Learning Objective 7.3.1

Group disability coverage

🩺Group disability — types & taxation

Covers sick pay, short-term disability, and long-term disability. Group DI generally has no riders, leans toward a modified any-occupation definition (more stringent than own-occupation), and is usually issued without health questions — its biggest advantage.

Who pays the premium drives the taxation of benefits
Who pays the premiumTaxation of benefit
Employer paysEmployee pays tax on the benefit
Employee paysEmployee does not pay tax on the benefit
Shared (each pays a portion)Employee taxed on a portion of the benefit
Who-paid ruleFollow the premium dollars: employer-paid premium → taxable benefit; employee-paid (after-tax) premium → tax-free benefit.

Learning Objective 7.4.1

Fringe benefits, workers' comp & unemployment

🎁Nontaxable noncash fringe benefits

  • De minimis fringe benefits — limited copier use, occasional business tickets
  • Working-condition fringes (must be business-related & substantiated)
  • No-additional-cost services · qualified employee discounts
  • Use of on-premises athletic facilities
  • Meals & lodging furnished for the employer's convenience
  • Commuter transportation & transit passes up to $325/month (2025)

🏗️Workers' compensation

Common themes: mandatory, negligence is not a factor, indemnity is partial but final, periodic payments, and premiums are a cost of production.

  • Benefits: medical expenses; disability (temporary total/partial, permanent total/partial); death benefits; rehabilitation
  • Benefits are NOT taxed

Prepaid legal services may cover bankruptcy, adoption, divorce legal fees, and estate-planning document preparation; the employer pays and deducts the cost of maintaining the plan.

🧰Unemployment insurance

Funded by the employer through federal and state taxes. Eligibility requires covered employment, minimum income earned, continued attachment, and involuntary unemployment (voluntary quitters and those discharged for misconduct are denied/limited).

  • Benefit amount is earnings-based via a state formula (roughly 50% of previous earnings, up to a state maximum)
  • Duration is set by the state — typically 26 weeks
  • A waiting period applies; benefits are taxed

Kaplan questions from this module

Key employee life
Which of these statements regarding key employee life insurance is NOT correct?
A. Policy premiums are tax-deductible · B. Death benefits received by the company are generally tax-free · C. The policy is owned by the company · D. The policy is applied for by the company
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ A — premiums are NOT tax-deductible

Key-employee life premiums cannot be deducted. The other three statements are correct — the company owns, applies for, and receives the (tax-free) death benefit.

GTLI · Table I
ABC provides GTLI equal to two times salary. Taylor earns $75,000; the Table I cost is $0.63 per $1,000 per month for Taylor's age. What monthly amount is included in Taylor's compensation?
A. $15.75 · B. $63.00 · C. $94.50 · D. $756.00
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ B — $63.00 per month

Coverage = 2 × $75,000 = $150,000. Excess over $50,000 = $100,000 → 100 units of $1,000. 100 × $0.63 = $63.00/month.

Group carve-out
Which group of employees is typically carved out of group term life insurance plans?
A. Older, higher-paid employees · B. Younger, lower-paid employees · C. <3 years of service · D. >3 years of service
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ A — older, higher-paid employees

Older, higher-paid executives are carved out of GTL (to keep plan costs down) and given individual, discriminatory coverage; nondiscrimination rules don't apply to them.

FSA
FSAs may provide funds to pay all of these except:
A. Deductibles on group health policies · B. Coinsurance on group health policies · C. A self-employed person's health-insurance deductibles · D. Dependent-care expenses
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ C — a self-employed person is not eligible for an FSA

FSAs are an employee benefit; the self-employed can't participate. The other items are all eligible FSA expenses.

Group dental
Which statement regarding dental expense insurance is CORRECT?
I. Coverage generally includes cleanings, fillings, crowns, and oral surgery. II. Many dental plans are offered through indemnity plans.
A. I only · B. II only · C. Both · D. Neither
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ D — Neither is correct

Dental covers cleanings, fillings, crowns, and orthodontia — but oral surgery is generally covered under health insurance, not dental. And today many dental plans are PPOs, not indemnity plans.

Unemployment
Which statement regarding unemployment insurance is CORRECT?
A. Paid under FICA shared by employers & employees · B. Voluntary receives same benefits as involuntary · C. Worker must have had unemployment taxes paid on their behalf · D. Benefits typically last 46 weeks
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ C — the worker must have had unemployment taxes paid on their behalf

Unemployment is funded by the employer (not FICA); voluntary quitters are denied/limited; and benefits typically run 26 weeks, not 46. The benefit is roughly 50% of prior earnings up to a state max.
