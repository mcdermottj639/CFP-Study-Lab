🔬Hazard → Risk → Peril

Three foundational terms the exam loves to blur together. Read them as a chain that ends in a financial loss.

The anatomy of a loss
TermDefinitionExample
HazardA condition that increases the likelihood of a peril occurring.Exposed / frayed electrical conductors.
RiskThe baseline possibility or probability of a loss occurring.The chance a loss lands past the probability threshold.
PerilThe actual event or circumstance that directly causes the financial loss.Fire, windstorm, theft.
Memory hookA hazard makes a peril more likely; the peril is what actually does the damage. Risk is the probability sitting in between.

🏗️Coverages A & B — the dwelling

  • Coverage B (Other structures): unattached structures on the premises — detached garages, sheds, fences. Limit formula: standardly 10% of Coverage A.

Inland Marine floaterProvides open-perils coverage for transported / valuable property at appraised value. It also solves the Pair or Sets problem: standard policies pay only for a single lost earring (destroying the set's value), while inland marine covers the lost value of the full set.

🧮The 80% coinsurance provision

The rule: a home must be insured for at least 80% of its replacement cost for partial losses to be fully covered. Otherwise the insurer pays the greater of ACV or the coinsurance formula.

FormulaPayout = ( Amount of insurance carried ÷ Amount of insurance required* ) × Loss − Deductible  (*required = 80% of replacement cost)

Worked example — "Zachary"

·
Facts. Replacement cost $400k · Required = $320k (80%) · Carried = $300k · Deductible $1k · Loss $20k.
1
Coinsurance ratio.
$300,000 ÷ $320,000 = 0.9375
2
Apply to the loss.
0.9375 × $20,000 = $18,750
3
Subtract the deductible.
$18,750 − $1,000 = $17,750
Insurer pays $17,750. Zachary absorbs the coinsurance penalty for being underinsured.

Liability

Section II — Coverages E & F

⚖️Coverage E — personal liability (CPL)

  • Definition: Comprehensive Personal Liability pays for bodily injury or property damage inflicted on others, plus legal defense costs.
  • Out-of-court: the insurer reserves the right to settle out of court without the insured's consent.
  • Who is insured: named insured, resident relatives, minors under 21 in the insured's care, and children away at college.
  • Note: excludes intentional injury, professional / business activities, and motor vehicles.
Exam trap — limit vs. liability (the bankruptcy trap)If a court awards $1M and the policy limit is $500k, the insurer pays $500k. If the insured then files for bankruptcy to escape the remaining $500k, the insurer MUST STILL PAY its $500k obligation — bankruptcy doesn't erase the insurer's duty.

🩹Coverage F — medical payments to others

Trigger: someone not living at the residence is injured there due to an act of the insured (applies on or off the insured property).

Who is covered — and who isn't
Result
Injured party is a guest / third party✓ Payout approved — negligence is NOT required. Limit ~$1,000 per occurrence.
Injured party is the insured or a household member✗ Claim denied — you cannot be legally liable to yourself.
Two quick examplesIf Alex's campfire burns his neighbor's house → Coverage E covers the damage. If George's dog bites a pedestrian → Coverage F covers the ~$1k medical bill.
The household exclusionCoverage F pays others, never the insured or household members — the key structural limit.

Umbrella

The Personal Liability Umbrella Policy (PLUP)

☂️How the umbrella stacks

A PLUP provides $1M+ in catastrophic liability protection on top of the base Auto/HO limits — which act as a deductible (the self-insured layer beneath the umbrella).

The drop-down penalty — "Harry"

·
Requirement. The PLUP requires a $300k underlying HO limit.
·
Reality. Harry carries only a $200k HO limit but buys a $1M PLUP. He is then sued for $1M.
1
HO policy pays its limit → $200k
2
PLUP pays, dropping down only to the $300k requirement level, covering up to the $1M total → $700k
3
Harry's personal gap — the $100k between his actual $200k limit and the $300k requirement → $100k out of pocket
$200k + $700k + $100k = $1M. Failing to carry the required underlying limit costs Harry $100k personally.

🚧PAP Part C — uninsured & underinsured motorist

Uninsured trigger
You're hit by an at-fault driver with NO insurance, or you're the victim of a hit-and-run. Covers the insured and anyone entitled to recover (e.g., a spouse).
Underinsured trigger
You're hit by an at-fault driver who has insurance, but limits too low to cover your total damages.
Action
Underinsured coverage must often be added by endorsement to make the injured driver whole.
ExclusionSettling with the at-fault party's company without your insurer's approval voids this coverage.

📎General auto provisions & endorsements

Subrogation clause
The insurer's right to recover claim payments made to you from the actual at-fault party.

Synthesis

Coordinating the P&C stack

🧩The planner's coordination job

Auto Part A liability and HO Coverage E liability form the base; the PLUP umbrella layer sits on top of both.

Risk management paradigm
Analyze property valuations & liability exposures, and coordinate policies to prevent overlap or catastrophic gaps.
Rule of thumb
Maintain liability limits at least equal to net worth (exception: high-earning professionals with debt need higher limits early in careers).
Coordination check
Ensure base HO & auto liability limits precisely meet the PLUP's minimum requirements to avoid out-of-pocket "drop-down" penalties.
Tie it togetherRemember "Harry": the umbrella only works if the underlying limits meet the requirement. The coordination check is what prevents a personal gap.

Which standard homeowners form provides open perils on BOTH the dwelling and personal property?
A. HO-2 · B. HO-3 · C. HO-5 · D. HO-8

✓ C — HO-5 (Comprehensive)

HO-3 is open on the dwelling but only broad on personal property; adding the HO-15 endorsement to an HO-3 upgrades property to open (an HO-5 equivalent). HO-8 uses basic perils with ACV valuation.

Coverage C limits
An insured's coins and bullion are stolen. What is the standard Coverage C special limit that applies to money, bank notes, coins, and bullion?
A. $200 · B. $1,500 · C. $2,500 · D. No limit
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ A — $200

Money/coins/bullion cap at $200. Securities & theft of jewelry/furs sit at $1,500; theft of firearms/silverware and business property at home sit at $2,500. Above these, use an Inland Marine floater.

Coinsurance
A home has a $400k replacement cost. The owner carries $300k (deductible $1,000) and suffers a $20,000 partial loss. Under the 80% coinsurance provision, how much does the insurer pay?
A. $20,000 · B. $18,750 · C. $17,750 · D. $15,000
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ C — $17,750

Required = 80% × $400k = $320k. Ratio = $300k ÷ $320k = 0.9375. 0.9375 × $20,000 = $18,750, minus the $1,000 deductible = $17,750.

Med pay inversion
George's dog bites a pedestrian who is not a household member, on George's property. Which coverage responds to the pedestrian's medical bill?
A. Auto Part B · B. Homeowners Coverage E · C. Homeowners Coverage F · D. None — negligence unproven
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ C — Homeowners Coverage F (Medical Payments to Others)

Coverage F pays others' medical bills regardless of negligence. Coverage E would respond to a liability lawsuit for damage. Auto Part B pays the insured, not others.

Umbrella drop-down
Harry's PLUP requires a $300k underlying HO limit but he carries only $200k. He's sued for $1M and buys a $1M PLUP. How much comes out of Harry's own pocket?
A. $0 · B. $100k · C. $200k · D. $300k
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ B — $100k

HO pays $200k; the PLUP drops down only to the $300k requirement and covers up to $1M ($700k). The $100k gap between his actual $200k and the required $300k is Harry's personal drop-down penalty.

Auto physical damage
An insured hits a deer, damaging the front of the car. Which Part D coverage responds?
A. Collision · B. Comprehensive (other-than-collision) · C. Uninsured motorist · D. Liability
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ B — Comprehensive

Contact with a bird or animal is a comprehensive (other-than-collision) peril, alongside fire, theft, glass breakage, wind, flood, hail, and vandalism. Collision is hitting a stationary object, being rear-ended, or overturning.
