Learning Objective 7.1.1

Calculate the education funding goal

🎯The three-move education funding calculation

Every education-funding lump-sum problem follows the same shape: inflate → adjust → invest.

🧮Worked example — Wayne & Mark (lump sum today)

Mark is 4; college starts at 18 (14 years out) for a 4-year program. Tuition today $25,000, college inflation 6%, investment return 8%. How much must Wayne set aside today?

Serial-payment method — INFLATE · ADJUST · INVEST

1
Inflate tuition to the first year of college (14 years).
$25,000 (PV) · 14 N · 6 I/YR → FV = $56,522.60
2
Lump sum to cover all 4 years at the inflation-adjusted rate, payments needed at the start of each year (BEG).
[(1.08 ÷ 1.06) − 1] × 100 = 1.8868 I/YR · 4 N · PMT $56,522.60 (BEG) → PV = $219,887.28
3
Discount that lump sum back to today (14 years) at 8%.
$219,887.28 (FV) · 14 N · 8 I/YR → PV
Wayne must invest ≈ $74,863.05 today to fund Mark's education.
Why an inflation-adjusted rate?The serial-payment method uses [(1 + return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1] as I/YR so a growing tuition stream and a growing investment are compared on the same footing.

Learning Objective 7.1.2

Estimating the Student Aid Index (SAI)

📄FAFSA and the move from EFC to SAI

  • The FAFSA® (Free Application for Federal Student Aid) is the common form completed for financial-aid consideration.
  • The old Expected Family Contribution (EFC) has transitioned to the Student Aid Index (SAI).
  • The SAI calculation is similar to the old EFC calculation.
Key changesPell Grant eligibility expanded; FAFSA simplified; the number of siblings in college no longer reduces the SAI.

📊What drives the SAI — income & assets

⚖️The asset-assessment gap, visualized

🧮Worked example — estimating a financial-aid package

Total annual college cost $65,000. Parent assets included at the maximum 5.64%; student-income protected amount $11,510. Parent income $150,000 (above the protected amount, counted at 22%), parent assets $175,000, student income $12,200, student assets $30,000.

SAI = [22%–47% income (p) + 5%–5.64% assets (p)] + [50% income (s) + 20% assets (s)]

1
Parent income — 22% of $150,000.
0.22 × $150,000 = $33,000
2
Parent assets — 5.64% of $175,000.
0.0564 × $175,000 = $9,870
3
Student income above protected ($12,200 − $11,510).
$12,200 − $11,510 = $690
4
Student assets — 20% of $30,000.
0.20 × $30,000 = $6,000
5
Sum to the SAI, then subtract from total cost.
($33,000 + $9,870) + ($690 + $6,000) = $49,560 SAI
$65,000 − $49,560 = $15,440 estimated aid
Estimated financial-aid package ≈ $15,440.
Reading the resultThe SAI is what the family is expected to contribute; the aid package fills the gap between total cost and the SAI. A lower SAI → more potential aid.

Iraq & Afghanistan Service Grants
For eligible surviving family of service members.
Other aid sourcesBeyond federal grants: college, nonprofit, state government, and private organization aid.

✅Federal grant eligibility — the checklist

  • Be a citizen or eligible noncitizen with a valid Social Security number
  • Hold a high school diploma or GED (or approved homeschooling)
  • Be enrolled as a regular student in an eligible degree/certificate program
  • Maintain satisfactory academic progress
  • Not owe a refund on a federal grant or be in default on a federal loan
  • Be registered with Selective Service (if required)
  • No drug conviction for an offense while receiving federal student aid

Learning Objective 7.2.2

Will the savings plan get there?

🧮Worked example — the Keaton family's 529 (4-step)

The Keatons invest $375/month into a 529 for newborn Annie. Current tuition $15,000/yr, education inflation 5.25%, 529 return 8%, college begins at 18 for 5 years. Will $375/month meet the goal?

INFLATE · ADJUST · INVEST · COMPARE

1
Future cost of the first year of college (END mode, 1 P/YR).
$15,000 (PV) · 5.25 I/YR · 18 N → FV = $37,678.11
2
Account balance needed to fund all 5 years — money needed at start of college (BEG mode).
$37,678.11 PMT · [(1.08 ÷ 1.0525) − 1] × 100 = 2.6128 I/YR · 5 N → PV = $179,037.80
3
Future value of the current $375 monthly payments (END mode, 12 P/YR).
$375 PMT · 8 I/YR · 18 × 12 = 216 N → FV = $180,032.30
4
Compare Step 3 to Step 2.
$180,032.30 − $179,037.80 = +$994.50
Surplus of ≈ $994.50 — the $375/month plan meets the goal.
Watch the mode & P/YRStep 1 is END, 1 P/YR; Step 2 switches to BEG (tuition is due at the start of each year); Step 3 is END, 12 P/YR with N = 216. Mixing these up is the classic error.

🔁Portability & control at the age of majority

If Student A no longer needs the funds, can the account be retitled for Student B?

  • 529s and Coverdell (CESA) — beneficiary can generally be changed (portable).
  • UGMA/UTMA — an irrevocable gift to the child; not retitled to another student.
  • Trusts — governed by the trust terms (e.g., 2503(b), 2503(c), spendthrift provisions).
Who controls the money?With UGMA/UTMA, the child assumes control at the age of majority (typically 18 or 21) even if education is no longer the goal. 529 owners keep control throughout.

Learning Objective 7.2.4

Gift-tax & planning implications

🎁Gift-tax rules and 529s

  • Contributions are completed gifts that can use the annual gift-tax exclusion.
  • Accelerated (5-year) contribution — also called ratable, superfunding, or 5-year gift-tax averaging.
  • A couple can combine their five-year accelerated 529 contributions and deposit $190,000 (2025) without gift tax.
  • Favorable estate-tax treatment — the account generally leaves the donor's estate despite retained control.
5 yr
Gift-tax averaging window for accelerated 529 funding
$190,000
Couple's combined superfunded 529 contribution, gift-tax-free (2025)

⏱️Why starting now matters

Three forces drive the funding math:

  • The persistent education inflation rate — costs keep climbing.
  • The persistence of time — compounding rewards an early start.
  • Changing the "N" value — more years of savings dramatically lowers the required monthly deposit.

Education tax credits — AOTC & LLC

💳The two credits at a glance

$2,500
American Opportunity Tax Credit — max per student (100% of first $2,000 + 25% of next $2,000)
40%
Portion of the AOTC that may be refundable
$2,000
Lifetime Learning Credit — max per family (20% of first $10,000 of expenses)
Per student vs. per returnThe AOTC is claimed per student (each eligible child), while the LLC is a single per-family / per-return credit no matter how many students.
