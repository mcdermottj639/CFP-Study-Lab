📋HIPAA — portability that fights "job lock"

The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA) sets major health-insurance-related provisions designed to reduce job lock — so workers aren't trapped in a job just to keep coverage.

RememberHIPAA is a federal law (not a program) that improves portability of coverage and limits how pre-existing conditions restrict people who change jobs or plans.

🔁COBRA — continuation coverage & durations

Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act (COBRA) lets qualified beneficiaries keep group coverage after a qualifying event. The duration depends on the event. A qualified beneficiary has a 60-day election window from notice to enroll.

🏛️PPACA — the Affordable Care Act

The Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (PPACA) expands coverage, impacts affordability, and sets standards for comprehensive health plans, through two mandates:

Individual mandate
Standards applying to individuals obtaining coverage.
Employer mandate
Standards applying to employers offering coverage.
Applies to…
Comprehensive health care plans only.
Exam trap — what PPACA eliminatedPPACA eliminated maximum (lifetime / annual) policy limits on comprehensive major-medical plans. It did NOT eliminate copayments, coinsurance splits, or deductibles — HSAs still require high-deductible plans.

🏷️ACA Marketplace — the metal tiers

Marketplace (exchange) plans are grouped into metal levels by how much of covered costs the plan pays — higher metal = higher premium, lower out-of-pocket. A separate Catastrophic tier is available to limited groups.

Medicaid & CHIP

🤝Medicaid & CHIP — coverage for eligible persons

Medicaid
For those unable to afford health care. Federally initiated, but administered & partially funded at the state level — each state sets its own qualification criteria.
CHIP
Children's Health Insurance Program — a federal program administered by states, for children in families who don't qualify for Medicaid.
ContrastHIPAA and COBRA are federal laws. Medicare is a federal program (mainly age 65+). Medicaid is federally initiated but state-administered welfare.
Medicaid

▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ D — Medicaid

HIPAA and COBRA are federal laws; Medicare is a federal program primarily for those over 65. Medicaid is federally initiated but mostly administered — and at least partly funded — at the state level.

Exam trapMedicaid is NOT a managed-care program — it's welfare funded by the federal government and each state. HMOs, PPOs, EPOs, POS and provider-service networks are managed care.

📖Health-plan cost terminology

  • Covered charges — expenses the policy will consider
  • Deductible — what the insured pays before the plan pays
  • Copays — flat per-visit / per-service charge
  • Coinsurance provision — the insured's percentage share (e.g., 20%)
  • Maximum out-of-pocket (MOOP) limit — cap on the insured's total spending; the plan pays 100% above it

💻Telemedicine & direct-to-consumer (DTC)

Telemedicine delivers health care remotely via telecommunications. Demand grew dramatically after 2020, and most major medical plans — including Medicare and Medicaid — now cover it.

DTC nuanceStandalone direct-to-consumer (DTC) plans are technically not insurance but cover basic services at a discount — an ideal supplement to a high-deductible health plan (HDHP).

Learning Objective 5.3.1

Calculating the cost of health care

🧮How MOOP math works

The insured pays the deductible first, then a coinsurance percentage of remaining covered charges, until total spending reaches the MOOP limit — after which the plan pays 100%.

HDHP / HSA contextHigh-deductible health plans (HDHPs) pair a large deductible with an HSA. The same deductible → coinsurance → MOOP mechanics apply.

🩹Worked example — Kayla's reimbursement

Kayla's major-medical policy: $1,000 deductible · 20% coinsurance · $5,000 MOOP. She has $25,000 of eligible expenses (first claim of the year). What does she owe, and what does the insurer pay?

Deductible → coinsurance → MOOP cap

1
Kayla pays the deductible first.
Deductible = $1,000 (insured)
2
Coinsurance runs until she hits the MOOP. She still owes $5,000 − $1,000 = $4,000 of coinsurance.
$4,000 ÷ 20% = $20,000 of charges at 80/20
3
Charges to reach the MOOP.
$1,000 deductible + $20,000 = $21,000 → MOOP reached
4
Everything above the MOOP is paid 100% by the plan.
$25,000 − $21,000 = $4,000 paid fully by insurer
Kayla pays $5,000 (her MOOP) · ABC Insurance pays $20,000.

Insurer check: 80% × $20,000 = $16,000 coinsurance + $4,000 above MOOP = $20,000.

MOOP calc
Richard's group plan: $1,000 deductible, then 20% coinsurance, $5,000 MOOP. First claim of the year is $1,500 of covered expenses. How much will his insurer pay?
A. $300 · B. $400 · C. $1,100 · D. $1,200
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ B — $400

Subtract the deductible: $1,500 − $1,000 = $500. The insurer pays 80%: $500 × 0.80 = $400. (Richard owes the $1,000 deductible + his $100 coinsurance = $1,100.)

📅Initial Enrollment Period — the 7-month window

Enrollment spans 3 months before the month you turn 65, the birth month, and 3 months after — a 7-month Initial Enrollment Period (IEP). Miss it and penalties, additional lifelong premiums, and pre-existing-condition issues may apply.

🅲Part C — Medicare Advantage plan types

Medicare Advantage (Part C) is delivered through private plans that combine Parts A, B, and D, often adding benefits not in original Medicare.

Learning Objective 5.4.2

Medicare supplement (Medigap) plans

➕Medigap — filling Medicare's gaps

Sold by private insurers as an alternative to Medicare Advantage, standardized Plans A–N carry different benefit levels. Basic benefits include:

  • Reimbursement for some costs not covered by Medicare Part A
  • The insured's Part B coinsurance percentage
  • All hospital expenses for an additional 365 days
  • Three pints of blood
Two rules to memorizeA senior may hold only one Medigap policy under current law, and insurers must accept all applicants within the first 6 months of Medicare qualification, regardless of pre-existing conditions.
Least-coverage planPlan A provides the least coverage at the lowest cost.
