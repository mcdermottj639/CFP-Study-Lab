Learning Objective 4.1.1

The three annuity types & their riders

🧱Three main types of annuities

Every annuity you'll be tested on is one of three flavors — the label describes how the cash value grows.

Fixed
Guaranteed, stated rate of interest — the insurer bears the investment risk.
Variable
Cash value sits in subaccounts; the owner bears market risk.
Indexed
A fixed value safety net plus growth tied to an index (via participation rate, caps, floors).
Preeminent benefitOf everything annuities offer, the preeminent feature is longevity-risk protection — guaranteed lifetime income, like Social Security. (Not creditor protection, not a guaranteed death benefit.)

✨Chief benefits of annuities

  • Tax-deferred growth during the accumulation stage
  • NO contribution limits or income phase-outs — unlike 401(k)s, IRAs, and Roth accounts
  • Protect against longevity risk — the risk of outliving one's savings
  • Their unique investment strategies can offer protection from bear markets
Exam trapAnnuity gains are ordinary income — there is no long-term capital-gain treatment. Highest-bracket clients benefit most from the deferral (a nonqualified annuity vs. a taxable brokerage account).

❓The six key annuity questions

Kaplan frames every annuity classification as answers to six questions:

  1. How are premiums paid?
  2. When do benefits begin?
  3. Who is the annuitant?
  4. What is the accumulation structure?
  5. What is the method of benefit payment?
  6. How long are benefits paid?

Watch the wording"Fixed" and "variable" refer only to the underlying investments. All deferred annuities begin payments at some point in the future — only an immediate annuity pays shortly after purchase.

📈Accumulation phase vs. payout phase

An annuity has two lives: money goes in and grows tax-deferred (accumulation), then is converted to a stream of income (annuitization / payout).

⏳Qualified Longevity Annuity Contracts (QLACs)

A QLAC is a deferred annuity funded with proceeds from a qualified account (IRA, 401(k)). It provides guaranteed income later in life to manage longevity risk.

$210,000
Max allowed investment (2025, indexed)
Age 85
RMDs can be deferred to (vs. normal age 73)
90 days

  • Married couples may continue joint lifetimes even after divorce
Exam trapQLACs do have contribution limits ($210,000 for 2025) and delay income to a later age (e.g., 80–85). They are not a liquidity source and do not guarantee higher returns — that's their whole design against longevity risk.

Distribution options & taxation

💵Annuitization (payout) options

How long — and to whom — the income is paid:

Straight life income
Largest payment; stops at the annuitant's death (nothing to heirs).
Life with period certain
Pays for life, but at least a guaranteed number of years to a beneficiary.
Life with refund
Pays for life; any unrecovered premium is refunded.
Joint & last survivor
Pays until the second of two lives dies.

🔗Equity-Indexed Annuities (EIAs)

A fixed annuity with two cash-value accounts: a guaranteed interest account and a variable indexed account.

  • Market-linked growth with principal protection
  • Guaranteed minimum interest during negative market returns
  • Upside tied to an external index (e.g., S&P 500), limited by caps, spreads, and participation rates
  • Similar interest-crediting methods as EIUL policies
Indexed mechanicsParticipation rate = the share of index gain credited · Cap = the ceiling on credited gain · Floor = 0% in a down market (principal protected).

🛡️Registered Index-Linked Annuities (RILAs) — buffer vs. floor

RILAs resemble EIAs (variable indexed account, similar crediting, caps & participation) but have no guaranteed minimum interest account. Instead they offer a buffer or a floor.

Buffer vs. floorA buffer absorbs the first X% of loss (you take anything beyond it). A floor caps your maximum loss at X% (the insurer absorbs anything worse). Higher buffers = more protection but usually lower upside.

🧾Annuity income taxation — the map

Two ways money comes out, taxed differently:

How annuity distributions are taxed
Distribution typeTax treatment
Nonperiodic (lump sum)LIFO — earnings come out first and are fully taxable
Annuitized (fixed or variable)Each payment split: return of basis (excluded) + earnings (taxable), via the exclusion ratio
Exam trapA lump-sum / withdrawal from a nonqualified annuity is LIFO — gains first, so it's taxed until you're down to basis. Annuitizing spreads basis across payments instead.

➗The exclusion ratio

For a fixed annuity, part of every payment is a tax-free return of your investment; the rest is taxable earnings.

Exclusion ratio = Investment in the contract ÷ Expected return

1
Numerator = investment in the contract (basis).
Jimmy's basis = $400,000
2
Denominator = expected return (payment × payments over life expectancy).
25 yrs × 12 mo × $2,000 = $600,000
3
Exclusion ratio = basis ÷ expected return.
$400,000 ÷ $600,000 = 0.6667
4
Excluded per year = ratio × annual payments.
0.6667 × (12 × $2,000 = $24,000) = $16,000 excluded
Excluded ≈ $16,000 · Taxable ordinary income = $24,000 − $16,000 = $8,000 / yr
After basis is recoveredOnce total life expectancy is reached, basis is fully recovered — any payments beyond projected life expectancy are fully taxable. If the annuitant dies before recovering basis, the unrecovered basis is deductible.

🚜Inherited annuities & 1035 exchanges

  • An inherited annuity not yet annuitized gets NO step-up in basis at death.
  • Spousal continuation is possible if the spouse is the sole beneficiary.
  • A non-spouse beneficiary is subject to the 10-year rule.
  • Tax impact can sometimes be mitigated via annuitization or a §1035 exchange.
  • Roth annuities (funded with Roth IRAs) can grow tax-free — less common than traditional.
Exam trapUnlike appreciated stock, an inherited nonqualified annuity gets NO step-up in basis — the built-in gain stays taxable to the beneficiary. Non-spouse heirs face the 10-year rule.
Exclusion ratio
Ralph, 63, used $700,000 to buy a nonqualified fixed annuity paying $62,000/yr for life; his life expectancy is 25 years. How much of each annual payment is taxable?
A. $0 · B. $28,000 · C. $34,000 · D. $62,000
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ C — $34,000 taxable

Expected return = $62,000 × 25 = $1,550,000. Exclusion ratio = $700,000 ÷ $1,550,000 = 0.4516. Excluded = 0.4516 × $62,000 = $28,000. Taxable = $62,000 − $28,000 = $34,000.

Suitability, risks & regulation

✅The suitability standard

Annuities pay high commissions, which create strong incentives and potential conflicts. Poor disclosures and widespread violations mean recommendations must be handled with care.

  • Suitability = aligning the annuity with the client's goals and risk tolerance
  • Per FINRA, suitability is whether a product/transaction is appropriate for the individual's specific financial goals, risk tolerance, and overall situation
  • A signed disclosure helps but does not, by itself, ensure protection
FINRA Rule 2111Cost must be considered — but the rule does not require recommending the lowest-cost option.

⚠️The subtle risks clients miss

Guarantees make annuities feel less risky than the stock market — partly true, but they carry quieter risks:

Inflation risk
Fixed payments lose purchasing power over time.
Opportunity cost
Locked-up dollars can't chase higher returns elsewhere.
Insolvency risk
The guarantee is only as strong as the insurer.
Mortality risk
Dying early can forfeit unrecovered value (depending on payout option).
Longevity examplePeter & Mary Jane ($225k nest egg) chose a straight-life joint-and-survivor immediate annuity with a 3% COLA rider paying $1,200/mo instead of drawing $1,787/mo (which would run out in 19 years). Both reached age 96 — with COLA, the payment had grown to $2,909/mo, and it lasts indefinitely.

💧Liquidity & time horizon

  • Limited liquidity — surrender charges and tax rules; some exceptions (free withdrawals, health-related waivers)
  • C-share annuities allow fee-free withdrawals but carry higher ongoing costs
  • Annuitization is irreversible — an opportunity-cost risk, though it also discourages impulsive behavior
  • Long-term products: benefits grow over time and are based on life expectancy
  • Optional features like COLA riders add more value the longer they're held
