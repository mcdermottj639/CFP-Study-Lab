Learning Objective 2.1.1

Homeowners forms, perils & other property coverage

🏠The standard homeowners policy — two sections

Every HO policy has two primary sections:

Section I — Property
Protects the dwelling, structures, and belongings (Coverages A–D).
Section II — Liability
Liability for acts occurring on the homeowner's property (Coverages E–F).
Homeowners coverages — Sections I & II
SectionCoverageWhat it protects

B — Other StructuresDetached garage, fence, shed

II — LiabilityE — Personal LiabilityComprehensive liability for others' injury/damage

🔥Perils — Basic vs. Broad vs. Open

Forms differ by which perils they insure against. Basic is a named-peril list; Broad adds six more; Open peril (all risks) covers anything not specifically excluded.

🏆Which form is highest? — the coverage ladder

HO-5 provides the highest level: open-peril coverage on both buildings and personal property. HO-3 gives open peril on buildings but only broad on personal property; the HO-15 rider (usually added to HO-3) upgrades personal property to open peril.

💎Other personal property insurance

Coverage for items excluded or limited on the homeowners policy:

Inland marine / personal property floater
Open-peril-level coverage for high-value articles: jewelry, furs, silverware, art. Covers items excluded or limited on the HO policy.
Flood insurance
Separate policy — flood is excluded on the HO form.
Earthquake insurance
Separate policy or endorsement — earthquake is excluded on the HO form.
Why a floaterHomeowners policies cap payouts on categories like jewelry and silverware. A personal property floater schedules those items for open-peril coverage at full value.

🏢Businessowners Policy (BOP)

The commercial cousin of the homeowners policy: a package policy that bundles property and liability coverage for small-to-medium businesses in one contract — analogous to how an HO policy packages Section I (property) and Section II (liability) for a home.

Learning Objective 2.1.2

Coverage amounts & costs — coinsurance

🧮The coinsurance penalty formula

To recover in full on a partial loss, the owner must carry insurance equal to at least the coinsurance requirement — usually 80% of replacement cost. Carry less, and recovery is reduced by the shortfall ratio.

✏️Worked example 1 — the $400,000 home

Replacement cost $400,000 · coinsurance 80% · carried $300,000 with a $1,500 deductible · loss $100,000.

Coinsurance penalty — step by step

1
Required insurance = replacement cost × 80%.
$400,000 × 0.80 = $320,000
2
Shortfall ratio = carried ÷ required.
$300,000 ÷ $320,000 = 0.9375
3
Apply ratio to the loss.
0.9375 × $100,000 = $93,750
4
Subtract the deductible.
$93,750 − $1,500 = $92,250
Insurer pays $92,250.

✏️Worked example 2 — Teresa's residence

Insured 10 years ago for $300,000 (replacement-value policy, $1,000 deductible). Today market value $550,000, replacement cost $500,000. Damage of $100,000 — how much does the insurer pay?

Use replacement cost, not market value

1
Required insurance = 80% of replacement cost ($500,000).
0.80 × $500,000 = $400,000
2
Shortfall ratio = carried ÷ required.
$300,000 ÷ $400,000 = 0.75 (75%)
3
Apply ratio, then deductible.
0.75 × $100,000 = $75,000 − $1,000 = $74,000
Insurer pays $74,000 (answer C).
Exam trapCoinsurance is figured on replacement cost ($500,000), NOT market value ($550,000). Using $550,000 gives the wrong required amount.

Learning Objective 2.2.1

Personal liability & umbrella coverage

⚖️Section II — liability coverages

  • Coverage E — Personal Liability: non-business, non-automobile personal liability plus medical payments to others; claim & first-aid expense; damage to property of others; loss-assessment coverage.
  • Coverage F — Medical Payments to Others: a separate additional coverage.
Med pay is NOT liabilityMedical Payments to Others pays for bodily injuries to others regardless of fault — typically up to $1,000 per person. It is not liability coverage and does not require negligence.
Dog-bite scenarioJackson's dog bites a pedestrian. Coverage E (Personal Liability) responds if he's found legally liable up to policy limits; Coverage F (Med Pay) can pay the injured person's medical bills regardless of fault. Both apply on OR off the insured property.

☂️Umbrella (excess) liability

Sits on top
Takes over after the underlying base coverage (auto and/or homeowners) is exhausted.
Requires a base
Must maintain required underlying limits on auto, home, boat, etc. first.
Catastrophic & cheap
A relatively low rate for a large amount of catastrophic liability coverage.
Sequence mattersYou must raise the underlying (base) auto/home limits to the umbrella's required floor BEFORE the umbrella will pay — you can't skip straight to umbrella on thin base limits.

Learning Objective 2.3.1

The Personal Auto Policy (PAP)

🚗Who & what is covered

Who is an insured: named insured, spouse, and resident relatives; anyone allowed to drive the covered auto; and a person/organization legally responsible for an insured's use of a covered auto.

Covered auto: any vehicle listed in the declarations; a newly acquired vehicle meeting PAP eligibility; a utility-type trailer owned by the insured (excludes mobile homes / large trailers); and a substitute for a covered vehicle.

🔤PAP structure — Parts A through F

The six parts of the Personal Auto Policy
PartCoverageWhat it does
ALiabilityThe biggest risk — bodily injury & property damage you cause to others
BMedical PaymentsMed costs for covered persons after an accident; funeral expenses; pedestrian/cyclist struck by a licensed vehicle
CUninsured / Underinsured MotoristProtects the insured when the at-fault driver has no / too little insurance, or in a hit-and-run
DDamage to AutoCollision (at fault or not) + Comprehensive / other-than-collision (fire, wind, hail, vandalism, theft)
EDuties after a lossInsured's obligations after an accident (prompt notice, cooperation, proof of loss, etc.)
FGeneral ProvisionsBankruptcy, policy changes, fraud, subrogation, policy period/territory, termination, etc.
Memory hookE = duties after loss, F = general provisions. Parts A–D are the coverages; E–F are the administrative "rules of the road."

🔢Split-limit auto liability — reading 25/50/10

Part A liability limits are usually quoted as three numbers (a split limit). Example 25/50/10:

$25k
Bodily injury — per person
$50k
Bodily injury — per accident
$10k
Property damage — per accident

State minimums can be as low as 20/40/10 or 25/50/15. A single-limit policy instead states one combined dollar cap for all liability in an accident.

Don't minimize to save premiumState minimums rarely protect real assets. If a client has assets to protect, raise base limits (e.g., to 250/500/100) BEFORE adding an umbrella — you can't buy umbrella on state-minimum limits.

🧾Part D detail & auto endorsements

Collision
Pays for damage to the covered auto whether or not the insured is at fault (e.g., backing into a fence post).
Comprehensive (other-than-collision)
Non-collision causes — fire, wind, hail, vandalism, theft; pays when insured is not at fault.
Endorsements
Extended liability, named non-owner, miscellaneous vehicles, motor homes, snowmobiles, antique/classic autos.

Coinsurance
Teresa's residence was insured 10 years ago for $300,000 (replacement-value policy, $1,000 deductible). Today market value is $550,000 and replacement cost is $500,000. On a $100,000 loss, how much does the insurer pay?
A. $59,000 · B. $60,000 · C. $74,000 · D. $75,000
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ C — $74,000

Required = 80% × $500,000 (replacement cost) = $400,000. Carried $300,000 → $300,000 ÷ $400,000 = 75%. 75% × $100,000 = $75,000 − $1,000 deductible = $74,000. Use replacement cost, not the $550,000 market value.

Liability
Samantha, golfing on vacation, slices into an adjacent fairway and injures another golfer (several thousand dollars of medical bills). Which coverages protect her?
I. Liability coverage   II. Medical Payments to Others
A. Both I and II · B. Neither · C. I only · D. II only
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ A — Both I and II

Med Pay would be quickly used up on several-thousand-dollar bills but is still available; liability coverage clearly applies too. Both respond, on or off the insured premises.

Auto policy
Raylan lives comfortably with assets to protect, in a state whose minimum auto liability is 25/50/15. His policy carries 50/100/50. Best recommendation?
A. Keep as-is, add umbrella · B. Raise limits to at least 250/500/100 · C. Do nothing — it's double the minimum · D. Cut to state minimums to save premium
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ B — Increase to at least 250/500/100

It makes no economic sense to minimize coverage with assets at stake — one injured party can exceed $50,000, multiple claimants can exceed $100,000, and $50,000 property damage is low for today's vehicles. Umbrella may help, but base limits must be increased before buying umbrella coverage.
