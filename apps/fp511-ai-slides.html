General Principles

The financial planning process & client life cycle

🔁The 7-step financial planning process

Definition: a collaborative, cyclical process that maximizes a client's potential for meeting life goals by integrating the relevant elements of their personal and financial circumstances.

Mnemonic (deck)Umbrellas · In · A · Downpour · Prevent · Immense · Mess → Understand · Identify · Analyze · Develop · Present · Implement · Monitor.
Red flagIf a client refuses planning when it is required, the planner must restrict the scope, terminate the engagement, or provide advice only (if planning isn't strictly required).

📈The financial life cycle — three phases

Definition: the three contextual phases of a client's financial life, driven by age, marital status, dependents, and income level.

ExceptionPhases can overlap and are indeterminate. A 60-year-old buying a car for a child momentarily exhibits Accumulation-phase behavior within the Distribution phase.

Conduct & Regulation

Advice vs. planning & the standard of care

⚖️Advice vs. planning — the integration factors

Definition: the threshold where standard financial advice transforms into a formal financial planning engagement requiring adherence to the Practice Standards.

Answer "YES" to any of these to trigger full planning requirements:

  • Has the planner agreed to provide planning?
  • Does the client reasonably believe planning is being provided?
  • Does the advice affect 100% of the client's assets?

The tipping "weights": number of relevant elements, % of assets affected, length of time, and risk exposure.

Red flagMerely executing a single stock trade for a client is Financial Advice, NOT Financial Planning.

🛡️Standard of care — suitability vs. fiduciary

Definition: the legal and ethical frameworks dictating how financial recommendations are formulated and disclosed.

Suitability standard vs. Fiduciary standard
 Suitability standardFiduciary standard
ApproachProduct driven (rules-based)Solution driven (principle-based)
BenchmarkRisk profile & time horizonBest interest of the client & all relevant factors
DisclosureVerbalWritten
RegulatorsFINRA / StatesSEC / States

Fiduciary duty requires: Duty of Loyalty · Duty of Care · Duty to Follow Client Instructions.

Duty to consultA planner cannot fake expertise. If a recommendation involves complex, unfamiliar tax law, the fiduciary standard mandates consulting a specialist.

Psychology

Behavioral finance & cognitive biases

🧠Cognitive biases

Definition: information-processing and belief-perseverance errors that cause irrational financial decision-making.

Cognitive
Faulty logic — can be corrected with education.
Emotional
Feelings-based — much harder to correct.
Three high-yield biases
BiasWhat it looks like
FramingIdentical mathematical choices are made differently based solely on how the question is asked (e.g., "keep $1,500" vs "lose $500").
RecencyHeavy weighting on recent events (e.g., expecting a 2008-style crash tomorrow because of a minor dip today).
Illusion of controlBelieving personal actions can dictate the outcomes of completely random events.
Red flagWatch nonverbal behaviors! Planners must give equal importance to how clients act (body language) as to what they say when identifying biases.

General Principles

Diagnostics — ratios, reserves & debt

📊Diagnostic dashboard — housing/debt ratios

Definition: standardized benchmark percentages lenders use to assess a client's capacity to assume additional debt. If either benchmark is breached, the client is flagged as over-leveraged.

≤ 28%
Housing Ratio 1 (Basic): PITI ÷ gross monthly income
≤ 36%
Housing Ratio 2 (Broad): (PITI + all recurring debt) ÷ gross monthly income
ExceptionRenters use a modified Ratio 1 that excludes property tax and home insurance. High property taxes can single-handedly ruin a client's ratio even with a low mortgage principal.

🔋Emergency fund thresholds

Definition: highly liquid cash/equivalents set aside to offset unexpected catastrophic expenses without risking principal.

How many months of reserves?
ReserveWho it fits
3 monthsDual-income married couples, or single clients with a massive secondary income source.
6 monthsSingle wage earners, or married couples with only one employed spouse.

Permitted vehicles: checking, savings, money market · CDs with maturity < 90 days.

Red flagsExclude one month's living expenses from checking-account calculations. Unsecured lines of credit / HELOCs are good backups but CANNOT serve as the primary emergency fund.

Time Value of Money

TVM mechanics & payment timing

🖩Master keystroke map

Definition: the mechanical application of compound-interest logic to future/present-value calculations using approved financial calculators.

Clearing the calculator before a new problem
StepHP 10bII+TI BAII Plus
Set P/YR = 11, DOWNSHIFT, P/YR2ND, P/Y, 1 ENTER, CE|C
Clear allDOWNSHIFT, C ALL2ND, CLR TVM
Clear againDOWNSHIFT, C ALL2ND, CLR TVM

The variables: N = periods · I/YR = interest rate (enter as a whole number, e.g., 4% = 4) · PV = present value · PMT = payment · FV = future value.

Red flagThe TI BAII Plus inherently uses cash-flow signs — it returns a negative PV when calculating an upfront cost. Always clear TVM memory before starting a new problem!

⏱️Annuities & payment timing (END vs. BEG)

Definition: a series of equal, periodic cash flows (PMT) where the timing of the deposit/payment drastically changes the compounding math.

Red flagIf a question says "starts today," "beginning of the month," or involves funding a child's first day of college, the calculator MUST be forced into BEG mode.

Education Planning

Funding a minor's education

🎓The 3-step education TVM engine

Definition: the chronological mathematical process to calculate the exact savings needed today to fund a future inflated cost.

Example — the inflation-adjusted (serial) rate

1
Given: return = 8%, inflation = 6%.
2
Apply the inflation-adjusted return formula.
[(1.08 ÷ 1.06) − 1] × 100 = 1.8868%
Use 1.8868% as the I/YR when discounting the 4-year serial tuition payment (in BEG mode).

📥Deep dive — Section 529 plans (QTPs)

Definition: a Qualified Tuition Program offering tax-free earnings growth and withdrawals for qualifying education expenses, controlled exclusively by the donor.

$10,000
K-12 distribution limit, tax-free per year
Unlimited
College distributions for qualified expenses
Gift tax
Contributions count against the annual gift-tax exclusion

Flow: post-tax inputs (any income level) → the 529 wrapper (tax-free growth) → qualified (tax-free) or non-qualified (taxed + penalty).

Red flagIf funds are withdrawn for non-qualified expenses (e.g., buying a car), the EARNINGS portion is subject to ordinary income tax PLUS a 10% penalty.

📦Deep dive — Coverdell ESAs (CESA)

Definition: a trust or custodial account created for paying qualified education expenses, featuring strict age and contribution limits.

$2,000
Absolute limit per year, per child — regardless of how many donors contribute
Age 18
Beneficiary must be under 18 when the account is established
Age 30
All funds must be distributed by the time the beneficiary reaches 30

Only one rollover allowed per year.

Exception to the age-30 ruleTo avoid forced distribution and taxation at age 30, the owner can change the beneficiary to another qualifying family member before the deadline hits.

🔒Irrevocable transfers — custodial accounts & trusts

Definition: legal structures transferring asset ownership to minors, removing the asset from the parent's estate but forfeiting parent control.

UGMA/UTMA vs. 2503(b) vs. 2503(c)
StructureHow distribution/control works
UGMA/UTMAAssets become the sole property of the child at the age of majority (18 or 21). No requirement to use funds for education.
2503(b) trustCurrent-income trust — income paid annually. Principal is never forced to be distributed at a specific age.
2503(c) trustPrincipal and income must be distributed to the child by age 21.
The risk — Kiddie TaxA 2503(b) trust keeps principal locked up, but choosing high-yield investments triggers massive annual taxes. Planners must select growth-oriented, non-income-producing assets.

📉Needs analysis — the Student Aid Index (SAI)

Definition: the formula used to evaluate eligibility criteria and a client's likelihood of qualifying for need-based federal financial aid. It calculates the income and assets of both the parents AND the children.

Reading the SAI
SAI levelFinancial needAid it points to
High SAILow needPLUS loans (non-subsidized)
Low SAIHigh needPell Grants, Work-Study, Subsidized Stafford loans
Huge testable exceptionParental home equity and accrued balances in retirement accounts (401k/IRA) are completely EXEMPT and are NOT included in the SAI calculation.

🎚️Synthesis — control of funds at the age of majority

Definition: the critical evaluation of who legally owns and directs the accumulated capital when a minor turns 18 or 21.

Final exam noteAlways prioritize the 529 plan if the client fears the child may decide not to attend college — the parent retains the full right to redirect or reclaim the funds.
