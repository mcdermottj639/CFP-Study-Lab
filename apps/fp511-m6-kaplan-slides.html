Learning Objective 6.1.1

Supply, demand & the price mechanism

🧭Economics — the study of scarce choices

Economics is the study of the production, distribution, and consumption of goods — i.e., how choices are made when resources are scarce.

Microeconomics
How individuals & companies decide to allocate scarce resources, prioritizing their respective wants.
Macroeconomics
The economy as a whole — e.g., the factors that affect a country's economic growth.

📈Supply & demand curves

Supply = the amount of a good available for purchase at a given price. The higher the price obtainable, the higher the quantity supplied — so the curve slopes upward left to right.

Demand = the quantity consumers want to purchase at a given price. If price increases, consumers tend to buy less — the curve slopes downward.

Movement vs. shiftA change in price is a movement along the curve. A change in factors other than price shifts the whole curve (S₀→S₁ or D₀→D₁).

⚖️Equilibrium — where the curves cross

Supply and demand are the two key determinants of price. Equilibrium is the intersection of the supply and demand curves. Prices should always move toward equilibrium unless restricted by outside sources.

🎚️Price elasticity — how much quantity responds

Elasticity is the degree of movement in quantity demanded in response to a price change.

Inelastic
Demand for necessities (food, medicine, gasoline) responds relatively little to price changes.
Elastic
Demand for luxuries (new automobiles) responds relatively more to price changes.
Memory hookNecessities = inelastic (you buy them almost regardless of price). Luxuries = elastic (easy to skip when prices rise).

🏭Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

Total monetary value of all goods and services produced within the domestic United States over a given year.

  • Includes income generated domestically by a foreign firm
  • Measured in constant dollars, which translates into real GDP after accounting for inflation (relative to a predetermined base/index year)
Exam trapGDP is location-based — it counts what is produced inside U.S. borders, including output of foreign-owned firms operating domestically.

Learning Objective 6.1.2

Monetary vs. fiscal policy

🏛️Two levers, two different drivers

Monetary policy
Conducted by the Federal Reserve Board (the Fed). Raises/lowers short-term interest rates because they affect consumer spending (demand).
Fiscal policy
Conducted by Congress and the current administration (president). Influences consumer demand through governmental policies (tax & spend).

🛠️The Fed's three monetary tools

The Fed seeks to control the overall money supply, thereby affecting future economic behavior, using:

Reserve requirements
Lower or raise the required reserves member banks must hold.
Open-market operations
Buy/sell government securities — the most frequently used tool.
Discount rate
Raise or lower the rate at which banks borrow from the Fed.
Open-market operationsBuy securities → increases money supply, drives interest rates down, expands the economy. Sell securities → decreases money supply, drives rates up, contracts the economy.

🏦Congress's fiscal tools: tax & spend

  • Power to tax — changes in tax rates affect corporate earnings, consumer disposable income, and worker incentives to produce
  • Power to spend — changes in government spending affect corporate earnings and consumer demand

Fed · Q1
Interest rates will tend to move downward when the Federal Reserve
A. is selling government securities · B. increases the discount rate · C. decreases the reserve requirements for member banks · D. approves lower personal income tax rates
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ C — decreases reserve requirements for member banks

Lower reserve requirements free up more money to lend, expanding the money supply and pushing rates down. Selling securities and raising the discount rate push rates up; income-tax rates are fiscal, not a Fed (monetary) lever.

Fed · Q2
Which of the following practices would the Fed conduct if it wanted to contract the overall economy?
A. Decrease the federal government's budget · B. Increase the prime rate · C. Decrease the reserve requirements for member banks · D. Increase the target federal funds rate
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ D — increase the target federal funds rate

Raising the target federal funds rate is a Fed action that contracts the economy. The federal budget is fiscal (Congress), the Fed doesn't set the prime rate, and lower reserve requirements would expand — not contract.

The business cycle & indicators

🔄Business cycles — expansion & contraction

Business cycles reflect movements in economic activity. The economy is usually in an expansion or a contraction phase.

  • The peak is the top of an expansion — its maximum
  • The trough is the bottom of a contraction
  • A cycle eventually reverses itself whenever a peak or trough is reached

📉Recession vs. depression

2 qtrs
Recession — real GDP falls for two consecutive quarters (min. 6 months)
6 qtrs
Depression — real GDP falls for six consecutive quarters (min. 18 months)
Nail the numbersRecession = 2 quarters / 6 months. Depression = 6 quarters / 18 months — both measured as a decline in real GDP from a baseline of zero.

Learning Objective 6.1.4

Inflation, deflation, disinflation & stagflation

🔥Four price-level conditions

Distinguishing the four terms
TermWhat it means
InflationContinued rise in the average level of prices of goods & services (the inflation rate is the rate of change in the general price level)
DeflationA decline in the general price level — the opposite of inflation
DisinflationPrices are still rising, but at a declining rate — a decline in the inflation rate
StagflationA combination of stagnation and inflation
Classic confusionDisinflation is NOT deflation. With disinflation prices still rise — just more slowly. Deflation is prices actually falling.

📊Inflation & deflation — the effects

Inflation
Each dollar buys fewer goods; raises the cost of homes, durables & consumption. If it outpaces wage growth → loss of purchasing power. Hits retirees on fixed incomes hardest.
Deflation
Decline in the general price level, often from a reduced money supply & consumer demand. A surplus of goods, a shortage of cash. Buying power rises initially — but is outweighed by rising unemployment & falling production.

🛢️Stagflation — the 1970s special case

Stagflation = stagnation + inflation. It occurs when inflation and unemployment rise together while overall economic growth is slow as business output falls.

It took place for a relatively short period in the 1970s, when rapidly rising oil prices caused consumer prices to jump sharply and businesses to cut back production.

Test tipFocus on the typical causes and effects of inflation, disinflation, and deflation. Don't overanalyze — for the CFP® exam, just focus on what typically should happen.

Bankruptcy & the 2005 Act

⚖️The three bankruptcy chapters

Chapter 13 vs. Chapter 7 vs. Chapter 11
ChapterWho / whatHow it works
Chapter 13Individuals with regular incomeAdjustment of debts — payments restructured (sometimes reduced) to be manageable. Debtor generally keeps their assets.
Chapter 7Individuals (liquidation)Personal unsecured debts are generally canceled. The 2005 Act significantly restricted its availability.
Chapter 11Individuals, businesses & corporate debtors (reorganization)Anyone eligible for Ch. 7 is eligible; debtors remain in possession and keep operating. Exceptions: stockbrokers, commodities brokers & railroads.
Quick contrastCh. 13 = reorganize & keep assets · Ch. 7 = liquidate & cancel unsecured debt · Ch. 11 = business reorganization, debtor stays in control.

📜Bankruptcy Abuse Prevention & Consumer Protection Act of 2005

  • Individuals with the ability to pay (as defined in the Act) must file under Chapter 13 instead of having debts canceled under Chapter 7
  • Consumer Chapter 7 use is limited to liquidating credit-card bills or loans not secured by a house or other asset
  • Ch. 7 filers must have their debt situation analyzed for eligibility and complete a credit counseling program before approval
  • Lenders must warn consumers about the dangers of paying only minimum balances on credit-card debt

Learning Objective 6.2.2

Consumer credit protection laws

💳Consumer Credit Protection Act (Truth in Lending)

Before extending credit, lenders must disclose:

  • The dollar amount of finance charges
  • The annual percentage rate (APR)
  • Other loan terms and conditions
$50
Max consumer liability per card for a lost or stolen credit card (less any amount charged)

📇The credit-reporting statutes

Key consumer credit & reporting laws
LawWhat it does
Fair Credit Reporting ActConsumers denied credit must be told which agency supplied the info; they then have 30 days to request a free copy of their credit file.
Fair Credit Billing ActConsumer must notify creditor in writing within 60 days of a billing error. Creditor has 30 days to respond and 90 days to resolve.
Consumer Credit Reporting Reform ActCredit reports must be accurate, relevant & recent; restricts file access to bona fide users; denied applicants must be told why and given the agency's name & address.
Equal Credit Opportunity ActProhibits credit discrimination (race, color, religion, national origin, gender, marital status, age, sexual orientation, or public-assistance income).
Fair Debt Collection Practices ActBars abusive collector practices (contacting at work if the employer objects, harassment, misleading practices). Courts may still garnish wages for a legal judgment.

🕵️Identity theft — FACTA & common scams

The Fair and Accurate Credit Transaction Act of 2003 (FACTA):

  • Consumers can get a free credit report every 12 months from each of the three national credit-reporting agencies
  • Individuals can place alerts on their file if identity theft is suspected or if deploying overseas in the military
  • Consumer information must be disposed of safely and securely
Phishing
Pretending to be a financial institution and sending spam / pop-ups to entice you to give up personal information.
Skimming
Stealing credit/debit card data with a special storage device when the card is processed.
