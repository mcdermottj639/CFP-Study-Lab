Learning Objective 6.1.1

Disability insurance — terms & riders

🦽What disability income (DI) insurance does

DI replaces a portion of earned income lost when the insured cannot work due to illness or injury — protecting the client's largest asset, their ability to earn.

30 days
Once benefits become payable, insurers are given as much as 30 days to process DI payments
Elim.
Elimination (waiting) period — from onset of disability until benefits begin
Benefit
Benefit period — how long payments continue (e.g., 5 yrs, to age 65, lifetime)
Two dials to setA longer elimination period lowers premium (the insured self-insures the gap); a longer benefit period raises premium (more risk transferred).

📖Definitions of disability — liberal to restrictive

How a policy defines "disabled" is the single most important term — it decides whether a claim even qualifies.

Exam watchThe source ties "most similar to Social Security" to the any-occupation (most restrictive) definition. "Modified any-occupation" is the middle tier — reasonably fitted by education, training & experience.

📉Loss-of-income (loss-of-time) definition

A fourth approach ignores specific job duties and pays based on income actually lost:

  • Benefit is based on the percentage of income lost
  • Compares post-disability income with pre-disability income
  • Ignores specific job duties — the dollar drop is what matters

🔄Policy renewal (continuation) provisions

From strongest guarantee to weakest — a favorite exam ranking.

🧩Other disability policy provisions

Residual disability
Pays a partial benefit proportional to the income actually lost after a return to some work.
Partial disability
A flat/partial benefit when the insured can work but not full duties or full time.
Presumptive disability
Certain losses (e.g., sight, hearing, limbs) are presumed total — full benefits without the usual proof-of-loss.

🏢Business forms of DI insurance

Business overhead expense (BOE)
Reimburses the fixed overhead of a disabled owner's business — rent, utilities, staff salaries — so the practice survives.
Disability buyout
Funds a buy-sell so a disabled owner's interest can be bought out by co-owners.
12–24 mo

White-collar

➕Disability riders

Waiver of premium
Premiums are waived while the insured is disabled — coverage stays in force.
Guaranteed purchase option (GPO)
Buy additional coverage later without new medical underwriting.
Cost-of-living (COLA)
Increases benefits during a claim to keep pace with inflation.
Social insurance supplement (SIS)
Pays a benefit that is offset/coordinated with Social Security disability payments.

Learning Objective 6.1.2

Selecting a disability plan · underwriting · taxation

🎯Disability income exposures

  • Personal loss of income — partial or full
  • Short-term vs. long-term exposure
  • Retirement & Social Security impact — a long disability also halts retirement saving and future benefit accrual

📋Underwriting — occupational classes

  • Insurers group applicants into occupational classes by risk
  • Limits on income replacement (you can't insure 100% of income — moral hazard)
  • Benefit amount and available term can vary by class
  • Earned income, taxation of income, and other income sources are all considered
Blue- vs. white-collarMoving toward white-collar generally brings a more liberal definition (more own-occ policies), a longer benefit period, and more riders — but benefits track income, they don't fall.

🧾Taxation of DI benefits — who paid the premium?

The rule turns entirely on who paid the premium with what dollars.

Income-tax treatment of disability benefits received
Who pays the premiumWith what dollarsAre benefits taxable to the recipient?
Employee (individual)After-tax dollarsNo — benefits are tax-free
EmployerEmployer's dollarsYes — benefits are taxable to the employee
EmployeePretax dollarsYes — benefits are taxable to the employee
One-line memory hookPay tax on the premium → benefits are tax-free. Skip tax on the premium → benefits are taxed. Employer-paid (or employee pretax) premiums produce taxable benefits.

⏱️The disability timeline

Blue vs. white collar
All of these are differences between blue-collar and white-collar disability coverage except:
A. Definition becomes more liberal toward white-collar · B. Benefit duration becomes longer toward white-collar · C. Benefit payments become lower toward white-collar · D. Availability of riders increases toward white-collar
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ C — benefits do NOT become lower toward white-collar

Benefit payments are driven by income, not classification, so they don't fall at white-collar levels. The other three are true: more liberal (own-occ) definitions, longer benefit periods (blue-collar often capped at ~5 years vs. lifetime for white-collar), and more riders available.

🧮Worked example — Marcus's group DI benefit

Marcus (single, supporting his widowed mother) needs $4,000/month after-tax. His group coverage pays 60% of base salary, premiums paid by the employer. He earns $100,000, of which $10,000 is bonus. Average tax rate 20%. What will he live on if permanently disabled?

Group DI: strip the bonus, apply 60%, then tax it

1
Find base salary — the benefit is on base, not bonus.
$100,000 − $10,000 bonus = $90,000 base
2
Apply the 60% group benefit to base salary.
0.60 × $90,000 = $54,000/yr = $4,500/mo (pre-tax)
3
Tax the benefit — employer paid the premium, so benefits are taxable.
$4,500 × (1 − 0.20) = $3,600/mo after tax
Marcus lives on ≈ $3,600/mo — below his $4,000 need (answer D).
Why the other answers failThey forget to subtract the bonus, forget to tax the employer-paid benefit, or both. At $3,600 he can't meet current needs, can't save for retirement, and inflation widens the gap.

Learning Objective 6.2.1

Long-term care — types, terms & riders

🏥Levels of care

From most intensive (medical) to least — the exam loves ranking these.

Levels of health care for long-term care
LevelWhat it is
Skilled careHighest level. 24-hour registered nurse available, under a doctor's supervision.
Intermediate careLess intensive nursing or rehabilitative care.
Custodial careNonmedical assistance with daily activities (bathing, eating, etc.).
Home careCare in the patient's own home — the first level that is not institutional.
Respite careA temporary replacement caregiver so the primary caregiver gets a break.

🔑Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) — the benefit trigger

LTC benefits typically begin when the insured cannot perform a set number of ADLs — or has a cognitive impairment.

The six ADLs
Bathing · Dressing · Toileting · Transferring · Eating · Continence
Cognitive impairment
Severe cognitive loss (e.g., dementia) is an alternate trigger, independent of ADLs.
Assistance standard
Care must be necessary for the health and maintenance of the individual.
2 of 6
Loss of 2 of the 6 ADLs (or cognitive impairment) triggers a HIPAA-qualified LTC benefit

📌Important LTC policy provisions

  • Level of care required for initial benefit payments to begin
  • Elimination period — waiting period from eligibility until payments begin
  • Benefit period and benefit amounts (daily benefit)
  • Renewability and time of underwriting; waiver of premium
  • Preexisting-condition waiting period; nursing-facility & home-health qualification rules
  • Inflation rider availability
Avoid this policyA prior-hospitalization requirement (benefits only after a hospital stay) is a red flag — such policies are to be avoided.
$50–$500
Daily benefit amounts range this wide — most fall between $50 and $150

✅HIPAA-qualified LTC insurance

A tax-qualified LTC contract meets federal standards, so benefits get favorable tax treatment.

Benefit trigger
Unable to do 2 of 6 ADLs for ≥90 days, or cognitive impairment.
Conforms to NAIC
Meets the NAIC LTCI model regulation; is guaranteed renewable.
Nonforfeiture
Offers guaranteed nonforfeiture benefits.
Per-diem taxationBenefits from a HIPAA-qualified per-diem (indemnity) policy are received income-tax-free up to the IRS daily limit; amounts above that limit are tax-free only to the extent of actual qualified LTC costs.
Levels of care
Which of these is considered the highest level of long-term care?
A. Custodial care · B. Respite care · C. Skilled nursing care · D. Home care

✓ C — Skilled nursing care

Skilled care means 24-hour availability of a registered nurse under a doctor's supervision. Custodial care is nonmedical (bathing, eating), respite care simply relieves the caregiver, and home care is the least intense — none is under a physician like skilled care.

Financing long-term care

🏛️Medicare vs. Medicaid for LTC

Government programs and long-term care
MedicareMedicaid
Role in LTCOnly limited LTC benefitsLTC for the impoverished

Medicare trapMedicare will not cover LTC if the patient's health is not expected to improve — it is not a long-term custodial-care program.

🤝Partnership plans & the financing options

State-approved Partnership plans let the pool of LTC coverage purchased be excluded from Medicaid spend-down requirements — you can protect assets equal to the benefits used and still qualify for Medicaid.

Self-pay
Retain the risk; reverse mortgage or assets fund care.
Traditional / joint LTC policy
Standalone LTC insurance (individual or shared).
Life or annuity + LTC rider
A combo (hybrid) product with an LTC acceleration rider.
Spend down to Medicaid
Deplete assets to qualify for Medicaid coverage.

⚖️Factors influencing how much LTC to buy

  • Cost of LTC in the client's area; desired goals of the individual
  • Financial security of a healthy spouse; family support available
  • Available assets vs. Medicaid planning; premium cost
  • Daily benefit amount offered by the policy; marital status
Not a sizing factorHIPAA qualification affects a policy's tax treatment, not how much coverage to buy — don't confuse the two.
Medicare & LTC
Which stipulation must be met for Medicare to cover the cost of long-term care?
A. Care needed full- or part-time · B. Patient's condition must be expected to improve · C. Need for care can be determined by the family · D. Care can be skilled or unskilled

✓ B — the condition must be expected to improve

Medicare will not cover LTC costs if the patient's health is not expected to improve. It covers only skilled care with an improving prognosis — not open-ended custodial care.

Sizing LTC
When determining how much LTC insurance to buy, all of these should be considered except:
A. Cost of LTC in the client's area · B. Marital status of the client · C. HIPAA qualification · D. Daily benefit amount offered by the policy

Local cost, marital status (household expenses differ once one spouse enters a facility), and the daily benefit ($50–$500, most $50–$150) all drive the amount. HIPAA qualification governs tax treatment, not the amount of coverage.

Learning Objective 6.3.1

Veterans benefits (VA) health provisions

🎖️The Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA)

The VBA is an agency of the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. Its primary function is to administer benefit programs for veterans, their dependents, and survivors.

Major VA benefits include:

  • Compensation and pensions; survivor benefits
  • Rehabilitation and employment service; education & career assistance
  • Home loan guaranties; life insurance
  • Medical benefits (health, LTC, vision, dental)
Exam trapThe VBA does not provide general financial and accounting assistance — that's outside its benefit programs.

🩺How VA coverage is determined

Coverage primarily relies on:

  • Priority group — assigned by severity of need
  • The advice of the VA primary care provider
  • Medical standards for treating the veteran's current condition
ACA noteVA health care is protected by the Affordable Care Act as qualifying "minimum essential health coverage."

📍Where care is delivered

9M+
Covered veterans
1,200+
VA locations nationwide
1 site
Care is normally provided at one VA location of choice

Care settings include: VA Medical Centers · VA Community-Based Outpatient Clinics (CBOCs) · VA Centers · VA Community Living Centers and/or Assisted Living · the veteran's own home.

📝VA eligibility

General requirements:

  • Military, naval, or air service; not a dishonorable discharge
  • 24 months of continuous service — or the full period for which active duty was required

Minimum-duty requirements are waived if:

  • Discharged for a disability caused (or worsened) by active-duty service
  • Discharged for "early out" or hardship, or
  • Served prior to September 7, 1980

Note: current/former Reserves or National Guard may qualify.

🔗Coordination & cost

VA health coverage can be used alongside other coverage — private insurance, Medicare/Medicaid, and TRICARE. What pays depends on the service:

Which coverage responds
Service connectionWho covers it
Connected to prior/current active dutyVA benefits
Not connected to active dutyOther coverage (private, Medicare/Medicaid, TRICARE)
  • Veterans may drop outside coverage — but doing so carries risks
  • Veterans may use an HSA or HRA to pay for VA/non-VA services
