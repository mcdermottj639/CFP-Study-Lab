Module 1

Principles of Insurance & Risk Management

🔥The anatomy of a loss

Three linked ideas the exam loves to separate:

Exam fact — insurabilityPure risk (chance of loss / no-loss ONLY) = insurable. Speculative risk (chance of loss AND gain, e.g., gambling) = NON-insurable.
Insurable risk requires…
① Large number of homogeneous exposure units (law of large numbers) · ② loss definite & measurable · ③ loss fortuitous/accidental · ④ loss not catastrophic to the insurer.
Risk-handling methods
Control: avoidance, reduction. Financing: retention, transfer (insurance is the primary transfer).
Trap — insurable-interest timingLife: insurable interest need exist only at policy inception (not at death). Property & casualty: at BOTH policy issue AND the time of loss. You have unlimited insurable interest in your own life.

🎛️The risk-management matrix

Four responses, plotted by how often a loss happens (frequency) against how bad it is (severity):

Exam factLow frequency / high severity → TRANSFER (insure). High/high → avoid. High frequency / low severity → retain or reduce. Low/low → retain.

⚠️Hazard diagnostics — intent vs. indifference

Moral vs. Morale hazard
Moral hazardMorale hazard
Definition: unethical conduct intended to obtain insurance or induce a claim paymentDefinition: indifference / carelessness because insurance is in place
Motivation: active dishonestyMotivation: passive carelessness
Example: arson; lying on the applicationExample: leaving a car unlocked with keys in the ignition ("it's insured, whatever")
Exam fact — trapdoorMORAL = dishonesty.  //  MORALE = carelessness. The third type, a physical hazard, is a tangible condition (icy steps, oily rags, faulty wiring).

🧱The anatomy of an insurance policy (DICE)

Every policy stacks four parts:

Declarations
Information provided by the applicant (who, what, how much).
Insuring agreement
The core promise — what is covered and under what exact conditions.
Exclusions
The boundaries — what the company explicitly will NOT cover.
Conditions
Rules of engagement — duties of the insured (notice of loss, protecting property).
Exam fact — subrogationAfter paying a loss, the insurer takes the insured's recovery rights against the responsible third party — so the insured can't collect twice for the same loss.

📜Contract-law characteristics of insurance

The four legal characteristics
CharacteristicWhat it means
AleatoryOutcome controlled by chance; dollars exchanged are substantially unequal (small premium vs. massive death benefit).
Adhesion"Take it or leave it" — drafted by one party (the insurer); ambiguities are construed against the insurer.
UnilateralOnly ONE party makes a legally enforceable promise (the insurer). The insured can stop paying at any time.
ConditionalThe insurer only pays IF a covered loss occurs and the insured has met policy obligations.
Exam fact — indemnityIndemnity restores the insured to the pre-loss position — no profit from a loss. The principal exception is LIFE insurance, which pays a stated face amount.
Voidable-minor exceptionA contract bought by a minor is fully binding on the insurer but VOIDABLE at the option of the minor — the insurer stays bound. (Competent parties is one of the 5 contract requirements: offer/acceptance, consideration, legal object, competent parties, legal form.)

🩺Underwriting metrics — mortality vs. morbidity

The two core actuarial metrics
Mortality (life insurance)Morbidity (disability & health)
Metric: probability of death across a given groupMetric: probability of sickness / disability
Prices: life insurance (mortality + investment income + expenses)Prices: disability income (DI), health, and waiver-of-premium riders
Exam fact — adverse selectionAdverse selection = higher-than-average risks seek insurance more than lower risks, threatening the insurer's assumptions. Underwriting is the process designed to control it.

Property & Casualty Insurance

🚨Negligence & liability trapdoors

Four elements of negligence (ALL required): ① a duty is owed · ② the duty was breached · ③ actual damages · ④ proximate cause.

Absolute / strict liability
Responsible for damages even with zero negligence. Trigger: extra-hazardous activities (blasting, wild animals) — and workers' compensation.
Negligence per se
Negligence is automatic because a statute protecting that class of people was broken. Trigger: speeding in a school zone and hitting a child.
Vicarious liability
Liable for another's acts because of a relationship. Trigger: an employer liable for an employee driving a company car.
Trap — contributory vs. comparative negligenceContributory: ANY fault by the injured party bars all recovery (strictest, largely abandoned). Comparative: recovery is reduced by the plaintiff's % of fault (most states).
Two more exam factsCollateral source rule: the defendant's damages are NOT reduced just because the plaintiff's own insurance already paid. Punitive damages are NOT insurable (they punish; special/economic and general/pain-and-suffering damages are insurable).

🏠The coinsurance penalty — the most-tested P&C calc

To collect a partial loss in full, the home must be insured for at least 80% of replacement cost. Below 80%, the insurer pays the GREATER of ACV (Replacement Cost − Depreciation) OR the coinsurance formula result.

80%
Of replacement cost — the coinsurance threshold
Partial
The penalty ONLY applies to partial losses
Greater of
ACV or formula result is paid

Worked example — $400,000 replacement cost, insured for $300,000, $20,000 loss, $1,000 deductible

1
Required insurance = 80% × replacement cost.
0.80 × $400,000 = $320,000
2
Ratio × loss = (Carried ÷ Required) × Loss.
($300,000 ÷ $320,000) × $20,000 = $18,750
3
Subtract the deductible (the classic missed step).
$18,750 − $1,000 = $17,750
Insurer pays $17,750 — the greater of this or ACV; never more than the policy limit.
TrapForgetting to subtract the deductible after the ratio math — the $18,750 distractor will be waiting for you.

Exam fact — VUL regulation tie-inBecause the policyowner holds the investment risk in subaccounts, VUL (and VL) is a registered security and MUST be sold with a PROSPECTUS.
Client-cue shortcutsWants guarantees → whole life. Wants flexibility → UL. UL Option A = level benefit (NAR shrinks as cash value grows, cheaper); Option B = face + cash value (NAR level, costs more).

📏How much? — HLV vs. needs approach

Human life value
PV of future earnings devoted to the family — an income-replacement figure that sets the upper bound.
Needs approach
Capital to fund survivor income + final expenses + debts − existing resources. Usually the exam-preferred method.
Exam fact — capital utilization vs. retentionCapital utilization spends principal down to zero over the income period; capital retention lives on interest and preserves principal for heirs — so retention requires a LARGER death benefit. The two broad economic assumptions of a needs analysis: rate of return and inflation.

💵Life insurance taxation — the four pillars

Tax-FREE
Death benefit to the beneficiary (but included in the owner's estate)
Deferred
Cash-value growth while the policy is in force
FIFO
Withdrawals: tax-free up to basis first (non-MEC)
Loans
Not taxable while the policy stays in force
Trap — lapse with a loan outstandingIf the policy lapses or is surrendered with a loan outstanding, the loan amount in excess of basis becomes immediately taxable.
Two more rulesDividends = nontaxable return of premium (but interest on accumulated dividends IS taxable). Transfer-for-value: selling a policy for consideration can make the death benefit taxable — exceptions include transfers to the insured, a partner, their partnership, or a corporation where the insured is an officer/shareholder.

🚨The MEC trapdoor (Modified Endowment Contract)

Overfund a cash-value policy past the 7-pay test limit (TAMRA 1988) and it becomes a MEC — permanently.

7-pay
Cumulative deposits exceed the net level premium in the first 7 years → MEC
LIFO +10%
MEC loans/withdrawals taxed gains-first, plus 10% penalty before 59½
Forever
"Once a MEC, always a MEC"
TrapsSingle-premium whole life is ALWAYS a MEC. Term can NEVER be a MEC (no cash value). And the death benefit stays income-tax-free even for a MEC — only lifetime access is penalized.

Riders & options to knowGuaranteed insurability: buy more coverage at set ages (usually into the 40s) with NO evidence of insurability, at the original classification. Best-growth dividend option: paid-up additions. Default nonforfeiture option on lapse: extended term insurance.

🏦The LIRP strategy (Life Insurance Retirement Plan)

Using a permanent policy (never term) to supplement retirement — mimicking Roth treatment with no income limits, outside ERISA.

Funding
Contributions made with after-tax dollars (no income limits).
Growth
Cash value compounds tax-deferred.
Withdrawal
Non-MEC withdrawals are FIFO — tax-free up to basis, then tax-free policy loans.
The two LIRP fatal flaws① Overfund into MEC status → LIFO + 10% penalty. ② Let the policy lapse with loans outstanding → the gain above basis becomes immediately taxable.

Four annuity types by who bears the risk

⏱️The annuity timeline

Accumulation hazardsSurrender charges (declining schedule; most contracts allow 10% free withdrawals/yr) and the age 59½ rule (10% penalty on taxable early withdrawals).
Decumulation hazards + exam factOpportunity cost (principal locked in) and superannuation risk (outliving money — which lifetime annuitization solves). IRREVOCABLE: once annuitized, you cannot reclaim the principal. Annuities protect against living too long; life insurance against dying too soon.

🧮Deconstructing the exclusion ratio

How to separate the taxable gain from the tax-free return of principal in a fixed payout.

Baseline data$100,000 annuity purchase · $750 monthly payout · 20-year life expectancy.

Math breakdown

1
Total expected return
$750 × 12 months × 20 years = $180,000
2
Exclusion ratio = Basis ÷ Expected Return
$100,000 ÷ $180,000 = 0.5556 (55.56%)
3
Tax-free portion of each payout
$750 × 0.5556 = $416.67 tax-free (the remaining $333.33 is taxable)
$416.67 tax-free · $333.33 taxable per $750 payment.
Exam factsOnce basis is fully recovered (year 20 here), payments become 100% taxable. If the annuitant dies with basis unrecovered, the remainder is deductible on the final return.

🔀Irrevocable settlement options

Straight life
MAXIMUM income for the annuitant's life; STOPS at death; nothing to heirs. (Risk: dying early.)
Life with period certain
Pays for life BUT guarantees a minimum period (e.g., 10 or 20 yrs). Die in year 2 of a 10-yr certain → beneficiaries get 8 more years. Pays less than straight life.
Joint & survivor
Pays while either annuitant lives — the LOWEST income per dollar. Survivor continuation of 100/66⅔/50% (lower continuation → higher initial payout).
Exam factStraight/pure life = maximum monthly income and the pure answer to superannuation (outliving assets). "Max income, no heirs" in the stem → straight life.

🧾Annuity taxation — the rules that decide the answer

LIFO
Pre-annuitization withdrawals: gains out FIRST (ordinary income) + 10% penalty before 59½
1035
Life → Annuity ✔ · Annuity → Annuity ✔ · Annuity → Life ✘ (one-way)

Traps① Mixing LIFO (withdrawals) with the exclusion ratio (annuitized payments). ② The annuity death benefit is NOT income-tax-free like life insurance — the gain is taxable (a spouse may continue the contract; a non-spouse generally faces the 10-year rule). ③ 1035 into life insurance is never allowed.
Why nonqualified annuities at all?No contribution limits and no income phase-outs — unlimited after-tax dollars growing tax-deferred (unlike IRA/401(k)/Roth).

Disability, Long-Term Care & Veterans

🦽The disability definition ladder

Broadest (most expensive) → narrowest:

① Own occupation
Can't perform the duties of YOUR OWN job. Broadest — highest premium.
② Modified own-occ
Can't do your own job AND not working elsewhere.
③ Any occupation
Can't do ANY job you're reasonably suited for by education/training. Narrowest private definition.
④ Social Security
Can't do ANY substantial gainful work in the national economy. Narrowest of all.
Exam fact — split definitionGroup plans commonly use own-occ for the first 2–5 years (24 months in group LTD), then switch to any-occ — designed to push return-to-work.

💰DI taxation — the single highest-yield rule

"Who paid the premium, with what dollars?"
Premium paid by…Benefits are…
Employee, after-taxTAX-FREE
Employer (or employee pre-tax)TAXABLE
SplitTaxable in proportion to the employer-paid share
Three waiting periods — don't mix themElimination period = after the disability, before benefits BEGIN (the "time deductible"; 30–365 days, longer = cheaper). Probation period = after issue, before pre-existing conditions are covered. Benefit period = how LONG benefits last (e.g., to age 65).
More exam factsBenefits are capped around 60%–70% of income (preserve the return-to-work incentive). Renewability ladder, best → worst: noncancelable (premium locked) → guaranteed renewable (class-wide raises allowed) → conditionally renewable → cancelable. Residual benefit = proportional to actual income lost; partial = fixed % for a short period.

Employee Group Benefits

👔Section 79 — group term life & imputed income

The first $50,000 of employer-paid group term life is tax-free. Coverage above $50,000 creates imputed income at IRS Table I rates.

Worked example — $250,000 coverage, age 52 (Table I: $0.23 / $1,000 / month)

1
Taxable excess = coverage − $50,000.
$250,000 − $50,000 = $200,000
2
Annual imputed income = (excess ÷ $1,000) × Table I rate × 12.
200 × $0.23 × 12 = $552
$552 of W-2 imputed income (reduced by any after-tax employee contributions).
Traps① Impute income only on the coverage OVER $50k — never the first $50k. ② If the plan discriminates in favor of key employees, those key employees are taxed on the ENTIRE employer-paid premium (no $50k exclusion).
Exam factsDeparting employees may convert to individual coverage within 31 days, no evidence of insurability. Employer-paid dependent life is tax-free only up to $2,000 (de minimis). A carve-out moves older, higher-paid execs into individual coverage (taxable to them) to keep the base plan compliant.

Business Owner Risk & Succession

🤝Business succession — buy-sell architectures

Model A — Cross-purchase
Mechanism: owners buy policies on each other.
Pros: surviving owners get a basis step-up; owners control the policies.
Cons: needs n(n−1) policies — messy beyond 2–3 owners.
Model B — Entity-purchase
Mechanism: the business buys one policy per owner (stock redemption).
Pros: simple — n policies; admin burden on the company.
Cons: NO basis step-up for survivors; control shifts to the business.
Trap — the basis step-upOnly cross-purchase gives surviving owners a basis step-up. Saying entity-purchase does is the classic wrong answer. Few owners + want step-up → cross-purchase; many owners → entity.
Exam factA first-to-die joint life policy can efficiently fund a 2-person cross-purchase; a second-to-die (survivorship) policy is for estate planning, not buy-sells.

🔑Key person, split-dollar & BOE

Key person insurance
The business owns, pays for, and is beneficiary — provides working capital when a critical employee dies. Premiums NOT deductible; death benefit income-tax-free.
Split-dollar
Employer & employee share the premium and the death benefit under a written arrangement — an executive-benefit funding tool.
Business Overhead Expense
Reimburses a disabled owner's overhead (rent, staff) — NOT the owner's salary. Max benefit ~2 years. Premiums deductible, proceeds taxable (the mirror image of key person).
Tax mirror trapKey person: premium NOT deductible / proceeds tax-free. BOE: premium deductible / proceeds taxable. Swap them and you've picked the distractor.
Professional liability pairE&O = financial harm (accountants, lawyers, financial planners). Malpractice = physical harm (physicians, dentists). The BOP packages property + liability for small business; the CPP for larger firms.

🧩Synthesis — the planner's risk mandate

The whole course in three jobs of a planner:

The legal foundation
Verify the 4 contract characteristics (Aleatory, Adhesion, Unilateral, Conditional), insurable-interest timing, indemnity/subrogation, and the coinsurance threshold (80% RC).
The personal shield
Protect against mortality (life insurance — needs approach), morbidity (DI ladder + LTC 2-of-6 ADLs / 90 days), and superannuation (annuitization + exclusion-ratio math).
The business fortress
Fund succession (cross- vs. entity-purchase basis rules), key-person capital, workers' comp (absolute liability), and verify carrier strength (A.M. Best A++).
The big ideaInsurance is the asset purchased with pennies to guarantee delivery of exact dollars at the moment of maximum liquidity crisis.

The policyowner holds the investment risk in the subaccounts, so VUL is a registered security and requires a prospectus.

1035 exchange
Which of the following is a PERMITTED tax-free Section 1035 exchange?
A. Annuity → life insurance · B. Life insurance → annuity · C. LTC → life insurance · D. None of these
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ B — Life insurance → annuity

1035 is one-way toward annuities: life → annuity and annuity → annuity are allowed; an annuity (or LTC contract) can never be exchanged into life insurance.

Annuities
Using the deck's baseline ($100,000 basis, $750/mo, 20-yr life expectancy), what is the exclusion ratio?
A. 50.00% · B. 55.56% · C. 44.44% · D. 62.50%
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ B — 55.56%

Expected return = $750 × 12 × 20 = $180,000. Ratio = $100,000 ÷ $180,000 = 0.5556. So $416.67 of each $750 payment is tax-free; $333.33 is taxable. After year 20, payments are 100% taxable.

Medicare
Long-term custodial (ADL-assistance) care in a nursing home is paid for by:
A. Medicare Part A · B. Medicare Part C · C. Medigap · D. Medicaid, after spend-down
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ D — Medicaid, after spend-down

Medicare does NOT cover custodial care (the most common LTC). Medicaid does — it's means-tested, requires spending down assets, and applies a 60-month lookback to transfers.

Disability
An employer pays the full premium for a group disability policy. Benefits the employee receives are:
A. Tax-free · B. Fully taxable · C. Capital gains · D. Tax-free up to 60% of income
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ B — Fully taxable

Employer-paid (or employee pre-tax) premiums → taxable benefits. Employee-paid with after-tax dollars → tax-free benefits. Split premiums → proportionally taxable.

LTC
Under a tax-qualified (HIPAA) LTC policy, the objective benefit trigger is substantial assistance with:
A. 1 of 6 ADLs, 30 days · B. 2 of 6 ADLs, 90 days · C. 3 of 5 ADLs, 60 days · D. Any ADL, indefinitely
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ B — 2 of 6 ADLs for at least 90 days (or cognitive impairment)

Don't confuse the probation period (before pre-existing conditions are covered) with the elimination period (after the disabling event, before benefits pay).

Group benefits
A 52-year-old has $250,000 of employer-paid group term life (Table I rate $0.23/$1,000/month). Annual Section 79 imputed income is:
A. $276 · B. $552 · C. $690 · D. $1,104
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ B — $552

[($250,000 − $50,000) ÷ $1,000] × $0.23 × 12 = 200 × $0.23 × 12 = $552. Only coverage over $50,000 is imputed.

Business risk
Four owners want a buy-sell where each owns policies on the others. How many policies, and what's the tax upside?
A. 4 policies, simplicity · B. 8 policies · C. 12 policies, basis step-up for survivors · D. 16 policies
▸ Tap to reveal answer
✓ C — 12 policies (cross-purchase)

Cross-purchase needs n(n−1) = 4 × 3 = 12 policies — messy with 3+ owners, but surviving owners get a basis step-up. An entity-purchase needs just 4 policies but gives NO step-up.
