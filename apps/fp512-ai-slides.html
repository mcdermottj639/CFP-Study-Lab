🧱The anatomy of an insurance policy (DICE)

Every policy stacks four parts:

Declarations
Information provided by the applicant (who, what, how much).
Insuring agreement
The core promise — what is covered and under what exact conditions.
Exclusions
The boundaries — what the company explicitly will NOT cover.
Conditions
Rules of engagement — duties of the insured (notice of loss, protecting property).
Exam fact — subrogationSubrogation prevents the insured from double-collecting by transferring recovery rights to the insurer. It lives within the legal bounds of these contract sections.

📜Contract-law characteristics of insurance

The four legal characteristics
CharacteristicWhat it means
AleatoryOutcome controlled by chance; dollars exchanged are substantially unequal (small premium vs. massive death benefit).
Adhesion"Take it or leave it" — drafted by one party (the insurer); courts favor the insured in ambiguities.
UnilateralOnly ONE party makes a legally enforceable promise (the insurer). The insured can stop paying at any time.
ConditionalThe insurer only pays IF a specific covered loss occurs.
Voidable-minor exceptionIf a 14-year-old buys life insurance, the contract is fully binding on the insurer but strictly VOIDABLE at the option of the minor until the age of majority.

🩺Underwriting metrics — mortality vs. morbidity

The two core actuarial metrics
Mortality (life insurance)Morbidity (disability & health)
Metric: incidence & probability of death across a given groupMetric: incidence rate of losing health or the ability to work
Pricing factors: mortality rate + investment income + expensesApplication: prices Disability Income (DI) and Waiver-of-Premium riders
Exam fact — adverse selectionAdverse selection = the statistical phenomenon where high-risk individuals (those expecting loss) aggressively seek insurance, threatening the insurer's mortality/morbidity assumptions.

Property & Casualty Insurance

🚨Liability trapdoors in tort law

Absolute / strict liability
Responsible for damages even with zero negligence. Trigger: extra-hazardous activities like blasting or keeping wild animals.
Negligence per se
Guilt is automatic because a law designed to protect a class of people was broken. Trigger: speeding in a school zone and hitting a child.
Vicarious liability
One party is held responsible for the actions of another. Trigger: an employer liable for an employee driving a company car.
Exam fact — collateral source ruleIf a 3rd party causes a loss, they must pay you IN FULL, even if your insurance already paid you. (An exception to the Principle of Indemnity.)

🏠The coinsurance penalty seesaw

To get full coverage for a partial loss, a home must be insured for at least 80% of its replacement cost. Fall below 80% and the payout tips to the GREATER of ACV (Replacement Cost − Depreciation) OR the coinsurance formula result.

80%
Of replacement cost — the coinsurance threshold
Partial
The penalty ONLY applies to partial losses
Greater of
ACV or formula result is paid

The formula breakdown — three steps

1
Ratio = Insurance Carried ÷ Insurance Required (80% of RC).
Carried ÷ (0.80 × Replacement Cost) = Ratio
2
Unadjusted payout = Ratio × Amount of Loss.
Ratio × Loss = Unadjusted Payout
3
Final payout = Unadjusted Payout − Deductible.
Unadjusted Payout − Deductible = Final Payout
Pay the GREATER of this formula result or ACV — and only on partial losses.

💵Policy loans & mechanics

Loan interest math:

In arrears
Interest paid at the END of the year (e.g., borrow $1,000 at 8% = $80 due at year-end).
In advance
Interest discounted upfront (e.g., $80 paid on day 1 = an effectively higher APR than 8%).
Direct recognition provisionWhen a policy loan is taken, the insurer removes those funds from its general earning pool — so it will explicitly reduce the DIVIDENDS paid on the borrowed portion of the cash value.
Exam fact — guaranteed insurability riderAllows purchase of more insurance at specific ages (usually into the 40s) with ZERO evidence of insurability. Issued at the original risk classification.

🏦The LIRP strategy (Life Insurance Retirement Plan)

Using a permanent policy as a substitute for a 401(k) to mimic a Roth IRA.

Funding
Contributions made with after-tax dollars (no income limits).
Growth
Investment gains compound tax-deferred.
Withdrawal
Life-insurance withdrawals are taxed FIFO (First In, First Out) — basis first.
The tax trapdoor (in the client's favor)The owner withdraws up to their total paid premiums (basis) completely tax-free; further income is accessed via tax-free policy loans. (Note: FIFO applies to non-MEC policies — a MEC would tax gains first, LIFO.)
LIRP fatal flawIf the policy is allowed to lapse due to excess borrowing, all prior deferred gains become immediately taxable as ordinary income.

Annuities

🏗️Annuity architectures (accumulation phase)

Four annuity types by who bears the risk
TypeRisk holderHow it works
FixedInsurer (all risk)Guaranteed minimum interest rate (e.g., 2–4%) plus a current excess rate.
Variable (VA)OwnerFunds in subaccounts (mutual-fund equivalents); designed to outpace inflation.
Equity-Indexed (EIA)HybridGuarantees principal (usually 0% floor) while offering upside tied to an index, limited by caps / participation rates.
Registered Index-Linked (RILA)BufferMirrors a stock index (e.g., S&P 500) but with no guaranteed floor; owner CAN lose money but downside is buffered. Regulated as a security.
Exam fact — RILA vs. EIARILAs do NOT offer the guaranteed minimum interest rate that EIAs provide.

📈Indexing math — EIA & RILA crediting methods

Four ways an indexed product credits gains
MethodMechanics
Point-to-point (ratchet)Locks in the gain at the end of each policy year; that end value becomes the new starting baseline (0% floor).
High-water markUses the absolute highest value within a set period (e.g., 3–5 years) to calculate credit.
Percentage changeCalculates from absolute beginning to end, ignoring all wild swings in the middle.
Spread methodSubtracts a fixed percentage (e.g., 2%) from the total index gain before crediting.
Exam factIf an index declines 10% during a term, an EIA point-to-point credits 0% (protecting principal) — but it also limits massive upswings via cap rates.

⏱️The annuity timeline

Accumulation hazardSurrender charges and the age 59½ rule (10% IRS penalty for early withdrawal).
Decumulation hazards + exam factOpportunity cost (locked in — can't access principal to invest elsewhere) and superannuation risk (outliving money — which lifetime annuities solve). IRREVOCABLE: once annuitized, you cannot reclaim the principal sum.

🧮Deconstructing the exclusion ratio

How to separate the taxable gain from the tax-free return of principal in a fixed payout.

Baseline data$100,000 annuity purchase · $750 monthly payout · 20-year life expectancy.

Math breakdown

1
Total expected return
$750 × 12 months × 20 years = $180,000
2
Exclusion ratio = Basis ÷ Expected Return
$100,000 ÷ $180,000 = 0.5556 (55.56%)
3
Tax-free portion of each payout
$750 × 0.5556 = $416.67 tax-free (the remaining $333.33 is taxable)
$416.67 tax-free · $333.33 taxable per $750 payment.
Exam factIf the annuitant lives beyond the actuarial 20 years, all subsequent payments become 100% taxable — the principal has been fully recovered.

🔀Irrevocable settlement options

Straight life
Pays equally for the annuitant's life; STOPS at death; zero residual value for the estate. (Risk: dying early.)
Life with period certain
Pays for life BUT guarantees a minimum timeline (e.g., 10 or 20 years). Die in year 2 of a 10-yr certain → beneficiaries get 8 more years.
Joint & survivor
Pays as long as either of two named annuitants is alive.
Exam factPure life / straight life provides the absolute maximum monthly income guarantee against superannuation (outliving assets).

Employee Group Benefits

👔Group-benefits threads in this deck

The AI deck has no standalone group-benefits slide, but two of its foundational facts apply directly to group underwriting and plan design:

  • Adverse selection is the central group-underwriting problem — pooling and participation requirements exist to counter high-risk individuals loading into a plan.
  • Mortality pricing (mortality rate + investment income + expenses) drives group life premiums, just as it does individual life.
  • Waiver-of-premium and disability income riders — priced on morbidity — commonly appear in group packages.

Business Owner Risk & Succession

🤝Business succession — buy-sell architectures

Model A — Cross-purchase
Mechanism: individual owners buy policies on each other.
Pros: step-up in basis for survivors; owners control the policies.
Cons: the math gets exponentially messy with 3+ owners.
Model B — Entity-purchase
Mechanism: the business itself buys one policy on each owner.
Pros: simplicity; shifts the admin burden to the company.
Cons: potential alternative tax treatments; loss of personal control.
Exam factA first-to-die joint life policy is often used to efficiently fund 2-person cross-purchase agreements without invading business capital.

🧩Synthesis — the fiduciary risk mandate

The deck's capstone ties the whole course into three jobs of a planner:

The legal foundation
Verify all 4 contract elements (Aleatory, Adhesion, Unilateral, Conditional); confirm subrogation / coinsurance thresholds (80% RC).
The personal shield
Protect against mortality (Life/VUL/LIRP) and morbidity (HIPAA 2 ADLs / 90 days); defeat superannuation (straight-life annuitization + exclusion-ratio math).
The business fortress
Shield the entity via entity / cross-purchase buy-sells; mandate Workers' Comp (absolute liability); verify carrier solvency (A.M. Best A++).
Exam fact — the big ideaInsurance is the only asset class purchased with pennies to guarantee delivery of exact dollars at the precise moment of maximum liquidity crisis.
